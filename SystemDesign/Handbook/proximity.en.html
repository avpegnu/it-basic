1. Ask whether objects move and what nearby means

Coverage before ranking

  • Candidate retrieval must not omit eligible businesses under an exact-radius recall contract.

Layered freshness

  • Business updates, hours, emergency suppression and device positions have different SLAs.

Location privacy

  • Design consent, retention, access controls and minimum necessary precision from the start.

The baseline finds relatively static businesses within a lat/lon radius, filters categories/open-now, sorts by distance or relevance, serves details and supports owner CRUD. Example targets are API p99 below 200 ms, 100 million DAU and ordinary listing visibility within policy-bound minutes/hours. Takedowns/security removals have stricter freshness bounds. These are illustrative requirements, not benchmarks.

Straight-line distance differs from two kilometers of walking or five minutes of driving. Travel times need road graphs/traffic and different serving. GPS has uncertainty, and coordinates need explicit units/CRS. Ask whether radius recall must be exact or recommendations may be approximate, how many results are needed and what happens when results are sparse. Moving drivers need different write-heavy freshness/expiry behavior.

2. Estimate index bytes separately from dense-query work

One hundred million DAU times five searches/day yields 500 million daily requests, about 5,787/second on average and 17,361/second at an assumed threefold peak. For 200 million listings, a hypothetical packed 24 B cell/ID/overhead entry gives 4.8 GB raw. This is not Redis/tree resident memory: allocators, structures, coordinates, multiple resolutions, replication and simultaneous rebuild generations can add substantially.

At one thousand candidates/search, the assumed peak requires about 17.36 million candidate checks/second, unevenly distributed between downtown and rural areas. One KB of details per listing is 200 GB raw before indexes/photos. Full-index replication is appropriate only with measured memory/build/update/network and query CPU budgets; replicas do not always scale linearly through shared detail stores or hot partitions.

3. Contracts fix units, filters and cursor context

Validate latitude [-90,90], longitude normalization policies, finite numbers, bounded radii, filter cardinalities and result limits. Location APIs/libraries do not always use identical coordinate order: PostGIS ST_MakePoint takes longitude before latitude. Swapped values can still form valid coordinates, requiring known-location tests rather than range checks alone.

Precise locations in GET queries can enter access logs, browser histories or analytics. The example uses a POST body with appropriate redaction/no-store policies; POST alone does not prevent body logging. Cursors bind locations, radii, filters, sorts and index generations; changing locations invalidates old cursors.

          4. Separate reads from index publication

          The location service covers query regions, retrieves candidate IDs/coordinates, exact-filters, checks eligibility, ranks and hydrates response fields. Hydration order depends on filter/ranker fields; do not fetch every photo for every candidate. Request workers can be stateless while the fleet still depends on versioned indexes, caches and freshness state.

          The listing service persists source records/outboxes; indexers apply CDC or build snapshots. A source-database spatial index is a valid baseline when workload/SLOs permit, before adding distributed index/cache fleets. Query plans, recall and measured latency justify architectural changes; geolocation does not inherently require custom geohash infrastructure.

          6. Return coordinates, statuses, hours and suppression; stale records may be excluded.
          7. Check distance/eligibility, then rank/top-K and return query-bound cursors and partial-result flags when applicable.

          Listing edit to search visibility

          1. The owner changes coordinates or hours with an operation ID.
          2. A transaction stores the new version and durable outbox.
          3. Acknowledge the source revision; visibility has a separate SLA.
          4. CDC/outbox publishing has retries and per-listing ordering.
          5. Moves remove old-cell membership and add new-cell membership by version; queries deduplicate IDs.
          6. Invalidate/update details by version and prevent stale refills.

          Details cache miss

          1. Batch-read IDs using keys with necessary locale/visibility context.
          2. Read authorized sources/replicas within allowed staleness; coalesce hot misses.
          3. Return versioned records and negative/deletion state under the contract.
          4. Hydrate and check distance/status without reviving suppressed listings.

          Publish a new index generation

          1. Build a snapshot at a source watermark and retain subsequent incremental changes.
          2. Validate counts, coverage probes and checksums, and replay updates before publication.
          3. Readers pin generations for requests/cursors under retention policies instead of mixing partial snapshots.

          5. Cells retrieve candidates; distances decide radius membership

          Geohash interleaves longitude/latitude bits and base32-encodes rectangular angular cells. Nearby points can have very different prefixes across boundaries, and exact filtering cannot recover points never retrieved. Conservatively cover the entire region, deduplicate IDs and check distances. The demo uses a planar fixed grid to isolate coverage from encoding.

          A center cell plus eight neighbors suffices only when radius and local minimum cell dimensions are proven to fit the cover. It is not universal across radii/latitudes. Cells smaller than the radius need more rings or a region coverer; longitudinal widths in meters shrink at high latitudes. H3 gridDisk measures hops, not an exact kilometer circle.

          6. Distance models, the dateline and bounding boxes

          A latitude/longitude box is a coarse prefilter whose corners lie outside a circle. Dateline-crossing longitude intervals may split into two ranges; near poles, radius/cos(latitude) formulas require singularity/large-cover handling. Use tested geospatial libraries rather than mixing degrees/meters. Haversine approximates spherical great-circle distances, not exact ellipsoidal geodesics or road distances.

          PostGIS geography ST_DWithin uses meters and a spheroid by default; geometry distances use CRS units. SRID 4326 geometry coordinates are degrees, so passing 2000 does not inherently mean two km. ST_DWithin performs index-aware bounding comparisons followed by distance checks; indexes do not replace query-plan and CRS validation. The SQL below is a baseline to inspect with EXPLAIN and workload tests.

          CREATE INDEX business_location_gist ON businesses USING GIST (location);
-- location is geography(Point, 4326); longitude precedes latitude.
WITH q AS (
  SELECT ST_SetSRID(ST_MakePoint(:lon, :lat), 4326)::geography AS p
)
SELECT b.id, ST_Distance(b.location, q.p) AS distance_m
FROM businesses b CROSS JOIN q
WHERE b.status = 'active'
  AND ST_DWithin(b.location, q.p, :radius_m)
ORDER BY distance_m, b.id
LIMIT :limit;

          Illustrative radius query without open-now/ranking/cursors or production query-plan guarantees.

          7. Quadtrees adapt to density but require depth caps

          A quadtree splits regions into four children when leaves exceed capacity, producing deeper dense areas and larger sparse leaves. Searches recurse only into intersecting nodes before checking point distances. Trees do not make globe distances correct automatically; use suitable projections or spherical designs. Region bounds and boundary ownership must consistently assign each point to one leaf.

          Duplicate/tightly clustered points may never satisfy capacity through subdivision, requiring maximum depths/minimum sizes and overflow buckets. Moving-object updates need remove/insert operations, synchronization/copy-on-write and rebalance/rebuild policies. Read-only trees are simpler than multi-writer trees. The demo varies thresholds, depth and clustering to show memory/overfull leaves, not performance.

          8. Compare spatial indexes by queries and operations

          Separate latitude/longitude B-trees can narrow bands or combine bitmaps depending on plans/selectivity, but do not guarantee efficient large/dense radius searches. Ordinary indexes are not inherently unusable: composite/range encodings can help, while spatial GiST/R-tree families address the problem directly. Choose using plans, recall and p99 under real distributions.

          S2 combines hierarchical spherical quadrilateral cells with Hilbert ordering. H3 uses a hierarchical mostly-hexagonal spherical grid with twelve pentagons per resolution; H3 is not a Hilbert curve. Hops, average areas and parent IDs do not prove exact metric-radius coverage; use appropriate library covering/traversal semantics and distance checks.

          9. Coverage, exact filtering, eligibility and ranking

          A validated nearby search

          1. Cover

            Cover the circle with appropriate geometry and boundary/dateline handling.

          2. Retrieve and check

            Deduplicate IDs and check distances, statuses and filters at appropriate versions.

          3. Rank and page

            Use tie-breakers, generation/query context and declared partial semantics.

          Track cardinalities/budgets at each stage; final top-K must not conceal poor candidate recall.

          Start with conservative covers, retrieve coordinates/versions, deduplicate business IDs, then check exact distances and eligibility before final top-K. Selecting K nearest and only then removing closed/category-blocked items can underfill results despite other eligible candidates. Overfetching/adaptive widening can help, but finite budgets may still preclude exact top-K; expose approximation/partial behavior under the contract.

          Open-now needs business timezones, exceptional closures and holiday hours rather than the phone timezone for every listing. Relevance may combine distance decay, quality, categories, availability and personalization, but popularity can crowd out nearby results. Define hard radius/eligibility and deterministic tie-breakers first, and measure relevance separately from coverage/latency.

          10. Update visibility and snapshot swaps need versions

          Owner interfaces may read source revisions immediately while public searches follow separate freshness SLAs. CDC/outboxes decouple writes from every search replica; monotonic listing versions prevent old retries from reviving deleted listings. Moves require old-cell removal/new-cell insertion plus query deduplication; incomplete updates can create missing or ghost listings.

          Full rebuilds capture source snapshots/watermarks, replay later changes, validate coverage/counts/checksums and atomically publish generations per reader. Fleets do not switch globally at one instant: requests/cursors need generation pinning or explicit expiry. Tombstone/urgent-suppression overlays must survive rebuilds to prevent stale snapshots reviving removed businesses. Cache invalidation must resist stale refills rather than merely deleting a key once.

          11. Cache entries and pagination belong to actual queries

          Coarse-cell candidate caches can share work across users before exact filtering at actual points. Caching final results solely by cell can give incorrect edge distances/radius membership or leak personalization/visibility. Context can include radius buckets, filters, languages, index versions and policies; dimensions fragment caches, so measure saved bytes/work. Sensitive personalized results do not belong in shared CDNs by default.

          Distance sorts need stable tie-breakers such as business IDs. Cursors bind immutable query/generation contexts and last scores/IDs; current-time or changing-rating ranks require pinned snapshots/times or documented duplicate/skip behavior. Mobile location changes must not append old-query pages to new queries. Do not silently widen a promised two-km radius: ask or return explicit expanded-radius metadata.

          12. Static listings differ from moving drivers

          One million drivers reporting every five seconds create 200,000 updates/second before heartbeats/reconnects, unlike hypothetical tens of owner edits/second. Dynamic indexes need sequence/time validation, stale-update rejection, atomic cell-membership movement and expiry/offline policies. Redis GEO members do not automatically gain individual TTLs from key expiry; use timestamp indexes/cleanup or suitable entity-state designs.

          Region/cell sharding reduces write concentration but boundary queries must read all relevant shards and deduplicate. Metro hotspots may need adaptive splits and bounded search fanout; raw geohash prefixes do not inherently balance load. In-memory current-state pings are appropriate only under explicit failure/recovery contracts, with durable trip/audit events handled separately. “Never persist locations” is also overly broad.

          13. Privacy and degradation are core behavior

          Collect the minimum necessary precision under clear consent, retention and audited access controls. Rounding logs reduces detail but does not anonymize repeated trajectories automatically. Avoid exact-coordinate/user-ID metric dimensions; aggregate over sufficiently broad regions and reconsider sparse areas. POST bodies/TLS do not prevent internal logging, so scrub traces, exceptions and third-party analytics too.

          A timed-out geo shard can omit candidates and produce incorrect nearest results; return partial flags or fail under the contract rather than claiming exactness. Details-cache outages need bulkheads/coalescing/admission rather than hundreds of DB calls per client. Stale coordinate versions can make exact filters check old locations; define authoritative version matching and drop/refetch policies.

          14. Interview questions on coverage and freshness

          A good design explains sufficient candidates, correct distances and bounded listing freshness. Validate small fixtures against brute force, test boundaries and inject update/rebuild faults before optimizing.

          Why can querying one cell then exact-filtering still miss results?

          Nearby points can lie across boundaries. Exact filtering only examines retrieved points; it cannot recover missing ones. Conservatively cover the circle, then deduplicate/check distances with dateline/latitude/large-radius handling.

          Are nine geohash cells always enough?

          No. Prove coverage using the radius and local dimensions. Large radii or narrow high-latitude longitude cells may need more cells. Use region coverers and trusted distances rather than average areas as worst-case bounds.

          Does a 200M × 24 B index necessarily fit in five GB RAM?

          That is 4.8 GB of packed arithmetic before engine/allocator overhead, coordinates, multiple resolutions and overlapping rebuild generations. Measure resident memory and CPU/update bandwidth; full replication is conditional.

          Does immediate owner visibility require synchronous fanout to every replica?

          Owners can read source revisions immediately while public search follows a freshness SLA. Use CDC/outboxes, versioned incremental indexes and lag measurements, with separate urgent-suppression bounds. Fleet-wide synchronous fanout can couple writes to slow replicas.

          Can a quadtree capacity of one separate every point?

          Not for duplicates or points closer than finite resolution permits. Bound depth/minimum sizes and use overflow leaves. Test boundary ownership and single-leaf ID membership; thresholds do not guarantee logarithmic depth for every distribution.

          Does filtering closed listings after selecting twenty nearest yield twenty nearest open listings?

          No: most could be removed despite eligible businesses farther in the candidate set. Apply eligibility appropriately or overfetch/adapt under declared budgets/approximation. Preserve radius limits, timezones and exceptional hours.

          Is a faster index refresh enough for moving drivers?

          No. Update rates, staleness, expiry, out-of-order sequences, cell moves and regional ownership change. Separate current-state serving from durable trip/audit histories, reject stale updates and cover shard boundaries.

          Does rounding locations in logs anonymize them?

          Not reliably. Repeated coarse points can form linkable trajectories. Use minimization, aggregation, retention/access controls and workload-specific privacy evaluation rather than merely reducing decimal digits.
