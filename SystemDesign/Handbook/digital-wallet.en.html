Designing a Digital Wallet: ledgers, available funds and recovery

Separate intent, posted/held/available balances, transit and decisions; compare ACID ledgers with deterministic engines and test incomplete snapshots, uncertain transfers and concurrent holds.

Follow monetary intent to explainable outcomes

  1. Identify and authorize

    Stable intents, exact amounts and current rights.

  2. Reserve and commit

    Availability, balanced entries and durable results.

  3. Resolve and project

    Transit decisions and versioned views.

  4. Recover and reconcile

    Complete snapshots, replay and independent evidence.

Preserve value, identity and pending responsibility between steps.

  • Balanced totals do not prove intent, recipients or spending bounds.
  • Results, reservations and pending decisions recover with monetary state.
  • Timeouts indicate uncertainty rather than authorize contradictory refunds.
  • Posted, held and available differ; reservation admission needs atomicity.
  • Start with ACID and measured scaling; event sourcing does not eliminate coordination.

1. Wallet transfers differ from card payments

User intent

  • Persist identities before submitting; timeouts do not create new intents.

Money authority

  • Availability checks and monetary mutations share safe authority.

Visible progress

  • Expose pending/held/completed states and versions rather than vague success.

A wallet manages stored value through funding, balances, internal transfers and withdrawals. Payments covers external charges and uncertain provider outcomes; this lesson focuses on the internal ledger, availability, pending transfers and recovery. The baseline uses a single currency with history/audit trails; FX, credit products and fraud-model implementation are separate, while funding/withdrawal boundaries remain explicit.

Distinguish accepted commands, reservations, in-flight transfers, completion and rejection. Acceptance proves only its declared boundary; completion requires the monetary commit protocol rather than a queued command or isolated debit. Display balances may lag, but spending uses an authoritative concurrency-safe state.

2. Conservation is necessary but insufficient

Balance journals within a currency under an explicit chart of accounts. Operator user balances are generally liabilities and bank cash assets; debit/credit direction depends on account type. The labs show simplified holdings rather than a complete accounting schema. Funding, withdrawals and fees need counterpart accounts, so user totals need not remain constant across external flows.

Also enforce spending bounds, correct parties/currencies, identified effects, single reservation resolution, complete postings, recoverable results and fenced owners. Duplicate transfers can conserve totals while charging twice; excessive holds can preserve posted totals while making availability negative. Conservation does not prove authorization, uniqueness or bounds.

Use exact integers/decimals with asset scale and overflow checks. Minor units are not universally cents or two decimals. Encode large amounts as strings and use appropriate database types. Rounding/fees create explicit postings rather than ignored floating-point residuals.

3. Size throughput without ignoring coordination

Assume a peak million transfers/second with at least two postings per completed transfer. At an illustrative 100-byte event, logical rate is 100 MB/s, or 8.64 TB/day only if sustained; RF three gives 25.92 TB/day before overhead. Peak is not the daily average, and real identities/results/workflow facts often exceed this assumed payload.

Measure local/cross-owner ratios, hot accounts, retained identities, snapshots/replay and projection backlogs. A hypothetical 100-byte retained result per command at a sustained million/second creates 86.4 billion daily results or 8.64 TB raw. Deduplication cannot default to an unbounded RAM set; durable registries and enforceable scopes/archival policies need explicit semantics.

Choose partitions from measured throughput and recovery bounds. A hot sender can remain serialized by its spending constraint despite extra machines. Display caching does not authorize stale spending. Evaluate latency, pending age and rebuild time alongside throughput.

4. Transfer APIs expose identity, results and freshness

Persist intent IDs before requests so restarts retain retry identity. Scope keys and fingerprint all meaningful fields. Identical retries return existing results; changed bodies conflict. Concurrent claims need atomic authority rather than disconnected cache/database checks. Define retry semantics separately for transport validation and persisted business rejection.

Status reads require authorized authoritative decisions. Balance/history expose amounts and versions, with bounded read-your-write waiting/fallback. Cross-partition cuts may need vectors rather than fictional global sequences. Use stable pagination and explicit pending states.

        5. One commit authority with multiple read views

        A relational baseline atomically commits entries, balance/hold updates, results and outboxes. A measured-scale deterministic engine still needs authoritative result history and replication. Queued commands differ from committed decisions, and independently written command/event logs need a recovery/publication protocol rather than assumed atomic arrows.

        The diagram separates monetary authority, coordination, transit, views, snapshots and external reconciliation. Trace local commits, pending transfers, versioned reads, recovery and funding. The pure offline labs assume durable decisions; they do not implement storage/consensus.

        Trace money, decisions and recovery evidence

        Component map; the complete ordered request paths are explained below.

        A ledger authority is the baseline, with state-machine engines as a measured scaling option. Projections do not authorize spending; cross-owner transfers need durable decisions/step identities, and statements provide independent evidence.

        Transfer within one authority

        1. Send identified intent with an exact minor-unit string.
        2. Authorize sources, fingerprints/limits and current ownership.
        3. Commit balanced entries, available checks, results and outboxes atomically.
        4. Return completion only after the monetary commit boundary.
        5. Apply identified versioned projections without duplicates.

        Transfer across owners through transit

        1. Create stable identities and pending workflows.
        2. Reserve/depart under current authority and stable step identities.
        3. Balance source decreases against in-flight value.
        4. Deliver identified credits under durable commit; timeouts do not cancel.
        5. Resolve destination credits and transit under a defined accounting contract.
        6. Expose pending/completed/canceled states rather than infer them from transport.

        Read with freshness

        1. Request balances/history with an optional completed-write version.
        2. Read authorized coherent cuts with bounded waiting/fallback.
        3. Use authoritative available-funds checks when required.

        Restore and retry after lost replies

        1. Publish committed snapshots with compatible retained tails.
        2. Load complete monetary/control state and validate integrity.
        3. Replay versioned committed tails without external calls in folds.
        4. Retry scoped keys for persisted results or payload conflicts.

        Fund, withdraw and reconcile

        1. Submit durable identified intents with potentially uncertain outcomes.
        2. Convert verified facts into identified postings rather than trust redirects.
        3. Independently compare journals/projections by cut, currency and account class.
        4. Match independent statements, fees, timing and external identities.

        6. Start with an ACID ledger when appropriate

        Relational baseline

        • Transactions and constraints align journals, results and outboxes.

        Measured bottleneck

        • Measure contention, coordination, log latency and recovery.

        Engine alternative

        • Deterministic engines add protocol, schema, snapshot and rebalance complexity.

        Within one database, deterministically ordered locks can protect availability and balanced postings/results. Unique scoped intent keys prevent duplicate claims. Conditional updates or serializable transactions are alternatives when constraints are correctly modeled; defaults do not prove every cross-row invariant. Retry whole failed transactions under the same business identity.

        Materialized authority balances must remain atomic with journals. Eventual cache updates alone cannot authorize spending. Use identified transactional outboxes for downstream work. Benchmark skew, indexes, durability and replication before adopting a more complex engine; there is no workload-independent transaction-per-transfer ceiling.

        7. Commands are requests; events are accepted facts

        Turn a command into a monetary result

        1. Authorize and identify

          Check source rights, fingerprints and ownership.

        2. Validate current state

          Check available funds, accounts/currencies and limits.

        3. Commit accepted facts

          Align entries, results and workflow intents.

        4. Apply and project

          Apply deterministic state and versioned views.

        Durable requests and monetary commits are distinct.

        Commands are validated against authoritative state before accepted facts are committed. Folds do not call clocks, randomness or networks; external facts are explicit versioned inputs. Single-threaded mutation reduces local races without eliminating ownership, cross-partition or storage concerns.

        Completion follows committed decisions/results rather than speculative memory changes. Failed commits require speculative-state reconciliation. Recovery folds accepted facts without repeating external calls or re-deciding old commands under new clocks/policies. Persist business rejections when stable retry results are promised.

        8. Snapshots must retain retry and pending state

        Snapshots pin committed cuts, ownership/schema versions, balances, reservations, pending decisions/steps and supported retry results. Publish verified bytes/manifests while retaining compatible tails and previous recovery points. Avoid speculative cuts; cross-partition recovery needs consistent cuts or explicit in-flight protocols.

        Restore ordered committed tails and detect gaps/version mismatches. Correct balances with missing results can duplicate payments. Expiry needs enforceable scopes/epochs or durable registries; arbitrary UUIDs do not reveal age. Retention changes must preserve declared retry guarantees.

        The lab compares snapshot cuts after completed and rejected commands. Preserved receipts return stable results or conflicts; dropping them at cut two duplicates a balanced transfer. Full replay from zero reconstructs identities, showing why recovery tests need multiple cuts.

        9. Cross-owner transfers need recoverable decisions

        Across authorities, local debits cannot atomically establish credits. Options include cooperating distributed transactions, escrow/reservations or durable sagas. Prepared states, decision recovery and availability trade-offs remain. Business holds still require atomic reservation and race-safe resolution.

        A debit-first flow balances the source against explicit transit value before a mutually exclusive credit/refund resolution. Destination timeouts are pending uncertainty, not proven failure. Refunding on timeout while allowing late credits risks duplicated value or negative clearing exposure. Steps need stable identities, decision evidence and ownership checks.

        Recipient closure, freezes, currencies, limits and outages need explicit policies. Validate/reserve destination capability where required, or retain pending obligations under documented resolution. Compensation adds audited movement rather than erase history. The lab assumes durable authority; real participants need their own persistence/ACK/recovery protocols.

        10. Posted, held and available have different semantics

        In a simple no-overdraft wallet, availability subtracts active debit holds; incoming pending credits need explicit spendability rules. Reserving differs from posting. Availability checks and reservations must be atomic: concurrent stale reads can over-reserve while posted balances remain nonnegative.

        Resolve holds by posting, voiding or expiry under one authority. Partial posting spends part and releases the rest with linked immutable identities. Expiry/confirmation races need a single terminal winner; clocks trigger checks rather than authorize obsolete workers.

        Pending totals are not extra posted money. Principal/fee reservations need partial-resolution and rounding rules. The fixed sixty/forty lab illustrates arithmetic rather than implement concurrent durable resolution.

        11. Freshness, audit and reconciliation use different evidence

        Authoritative spend

        • Current availability, committed decisions and fenced ownership.

        Derived views

        • Rebuildable views with applied versions and freshness.

        Independent evidence

        • Statements and independent recomputation detect different error classes.

        Views apply identified versioned facts before advancing cursors. Completed-write tokens support bounded catch-up or fallback rather than pretend notifications are applied state. Rebuilds need exact cuts and reversal/version rules. Label stale displays while authorizing spends against current monetary state.

        Independently recompute journals, transfer completeness, identities and reservations, comparing views at compatible cuts. External statements provide independent evidence with fees, timing and corrections; they are not merely lower-trust projections. Investigate discrepancies and record justified corrections rather than blindly privilege a log or overwrite balances.

        12. Funding and withdrawals connect monetary systems through pending state

        Do not fund wallets from redirects or sent requests alone. Persist identified intents/outboxes, verify provider facts and post counterpart entries under explicit acceptance/settlement policies. Authorization, capture and settlement differ, while reversals need policy. Resolve uncertainty through stable identities and reconciliation rather than new charges.

        Withdrawals reserve/debit under a chosen model and track payout progress. Proven failures may compensate, while unknown states remain pending. Fees, FX and timing require counterpart accounts. Reconcile duplicate/missing/out-of-order facts and delayed returns; callbacks require verification and state rules.

        13. Hot accounts and migration must preserve spending constraints

        Receive-heavy merchants can use buckets if delayed aggregate visibility is acceptable. Debits need spending rights/escrow rather than stale aggregate sums. Batching trades latency while retaining identities. Asynchronous credits require durable obligations and valid resolution conditions.

        Migration carries committed cuts, tails, pending workflows and retry state before fenced cutover. Preserve intent IDs through routing refreshes. Locality reduces some coordination at operational cost. DR specifies loss/recovery objectives and prevents split authority rather than label stale restores loss-free.

        14. Model entries together with decisions

        Model accounts, scoped intents/fingerprints, entries, reservations/resolutions, workflow steps and outboxes, with compatible balance versions. Identity/reference constraints and commit protocols establish completion; eventual analytics records alone do not.

        The illustrative schema encodes exact amounts/versions as strings. Versioned replay/upcasters preserve economic meaning and are tested against archived facts. Partition sequences are not global order; archives still need lifecycle, access and restore design.

        15. Authorization and audit controls belong in the commit path

        Authorize sources/delegations and enforce monetary limits at authority; IDs are not capabilities. Risk/approval checks occur outside replay folds with recorded versioned facts. Protect sensitive facts, snapshots and exports; identity keys and status lookups must not leak secrets or account relationships.

        Adjustments/refunds need authorized identified counterpart postings and reasons rather than untracked balance edits. Apply scoped roles, encryption, rotation and monitored emergency access under product requirements; a schema alone does not establish every jurisdictional obligation.

        16. Test lost replies, stale owners and incomplete snapshots

        Monitor transfer outcomes/age, transit exposure, availability violations, identity conflicts, view lag, restores, outboxes and currency-specific reconciliation gaps. Trace identities without leaking payloads. Escalate pending work according to state rather than automatic blanket refunds.

        Inject journal/reply crashes, duplicate/conflicting keys, incomplete snapshots, coordinator/message gaps, late credits, reservation races, obsolete writers and provider retries. Check monetary/result equivalence and unique resolutions; balanced sums alone miss duplicated intent.

        17. Practice using monetary states and recovery evidence

        Draw a transfer through transit with lost replies, coordinator restart, destination outage and client retries. Identify committed facts, identities, irreversible decisions, spendability and snapshot contents. Then change payload/currency under the same key and test whether total conservation is sufficient.

        Why can total one hundred still be wrong?

        Duplicates, excessive holds and wrong recipients can preserve totals. Test identities, authority, amounts, bounds and completion separately.

        Are balances and a sequence enough for snapshots?

        Not when recovery needs results, reservations, decisions, steps and versions. Missing identities can duplicate payments after an apparently correct restore.

        Does an acknowledged event log append establish transfer completion?

        Only if the event/protocol commits all required monetary effects/results. Queued commands and isolated debits do not prove cross-owner completion.

        Can destination timeouts trigger immediate refunds?

        Not from a timeout alone. Preserve pending/transit state and resolve under mutually exclusive decisions and participant guards.

        When should event sourcing replace a relational baseline?

        When measured needs justify operational complexity. Evaluate contention, skew, coordination and recovery rather than target RPS alone.

        Does a single-threaded engine eliminate every lock and deadlock?

        It serializes local mutations without removing coordination, migrations, storage concurrency or durable authority requirements.

        Must the internal log always win over external statements?

        No. Statements are independent evidence. Investigate timing and incorrect/missing facts, then record justified corrections.

        How does posting forty from a sixty-unit hold affect availability?

        Posted one hundred with a sixty hold leaves forty available. Posting forty clears the hold and releases twenty unused, leaving sixty available. Resolution must have a single winner.
