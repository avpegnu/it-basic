1. A real-time marketplace beyond map dots

Fast projections

  • Locations, ETAs and candidates need freshness, bounded latency and explicit staleness.

Durable invariants

  • Trips, reservations and results need consistency, recovery and unique effects.

Marketplace objective

  • Total wait, tails, acceptance, fairness and coverage can conflict.

We build ride-hailing in an Uber-like product context: quotes, requests, candidates, offers, acceptance, trip progress, cancellation/completion and payment/receipt events. This chapter assumes non-pooling with at most one active trip per driver. Pooling, multi-job couriers and airport queues require different constraints.

Public work on H3, domain-oriented architecture and transactional fulfillment illustrates changing pressures. The architecture below is AV Lore’s teaching synthesis, not a claim about current private topology or real traffic. Stages follow learning pressures rather than a mandatory startup technology sequence.

2. Write invariants before drawing matching

Three proofs before committing assignment

  1. Valid trip

    State/version still permits acceptance.

  2. Current offer

    Tokens, generations, sessions and expiry remain valid.

  3. Eligible driver

    Reserve atomically under capacity/ownership constraints.

  4. Commit and replay

    Persist results/outbox; retries retain logical identity.

Offer delivery and UI do not establish these proofs.

One trip has at most one committed driver; one driver has at most one active reservation/trip. Offers bind trips, sessions, generations and expiry; repeated logical acceptance returns retained results. A CAS on one trip protects that trip but not a driver across two trip IDs. Use supported atomic transactions or equivalent reservation/fencing protocols.

A teaching lifecycle moves through requested, offered, assigned, arriving, in-progress and completed/canceled with guarded transitions. Cancellation, expiry and completion can race, so use expected versions and retained outcomes. Release only matching reservation ownership; delayed cancellation must not free a driver reassigned to another trip.

3. 12 stages: change design under measured pressure

Trace stages from hot paths and durable cores to recovery, contracts, tracing, streams, ML and domains. Each stage explains pressures, decisions, trade-offs and exit criteria. Sharing a trip ID does not make location, history and payments share durability/freshness contracts.

One app and one trip authority

Start in a small region: riders request, drivers report availability/locations, offers become trips. One modular app and relational store may suffice; spatial indexing avoids scans. This chapter assumes non-pooling, with at most one active trip per driver. Trips and reservations need consistency; map dots are projections.

Problem pressure: A small team needs working, debuggable request, acceptance and cancellation.

Architectural decision: Use modular APIs, transactional trip/driver state, scoped command IDs and bounded notification.

Costs and trade-offs: GPS writes compete with trip transactions and releases have broad impact.

When is the next step needed?: Measured location or dispatch limits exceed SLOs, not merely more cities.

Retries do not create duplicate trips; concurrent accepts respect both uniqueness constraints and versioned cancellation.

Simple schemas still need indexes, transactions and recovery; relational storage does not imply scanning every driver.

Recover trips and mobile sessions

Replicas, backups, fencing and drills underpin recovery. Mobile versions, command results and protected capsules can aid reconnect after authentication, scope, version, expiry and reconciliation. Architectural decision: Combine durable failover contracts with versioned reconnect snapshots/status and replay-safe pending commands.

Costs and trade-offs: Synchronous replication costs latency; asynchronous copies have RPO and client hints cannot restore every committed write.

4. Separate location, matching and durable-write volume

Assume two million online drivers, one update every five seconds and 150 raw bytes/fix. That is 400 thousand fixes/second and 60 MB/second before transport, indexes and replicas. Latest raw state is 300 MB, but real memory includes sessions, memberships, allocation/GC and replication. RAM fit does not establish single-process throughput or resilience.

At twenty million trips/day, eight transitions each and sixfold peaks, average transitions are about 1,852/second and peak 11,111. These are transitions, not all DB transactions or payments. At 2,000 requests/second and forty candidates each, naive dispatch needs 80,000 ETA evaluations/second before batching/caching/filtering. Bound dense-cell fan-out and deadlines.

Retaining 400K fixes/second for one day yields 34.56 billion fixes or 5.184 decimal TB before overhead/replicas/compression. Latest caches and purpose-specific history are meaningful cost/privacy choices. Offline and delayed reports prevent promising full fleet refill in exactly five seconds.

5. Trace requests, offers, acceptance and reconnect

Supply traces maintain projections; request traces treat candidates/ETA as suggestions. Drivers can go offline or take other work before acceptance. Only authoritative commits establish assignment. Sent, delivered, seen and committed are distinct acknowledgments.

Completion persists event intent before side effects. Retried transaction callbacks must not directly charge or perform non-idempotent notifications. Reconnect returns snapshots/cursors and retained results; old updates cannot regress trips. If authority is recovering, expose that state instead of inventing assignments from map caches.

6. Latest values need authenticated epochs, sequences and TTLs

Later arrival does not imply newer data. Server-issued active epochs and session sequences reject stale packets; clients cannot invent epochs. Duplicates must not refresh receipt time as new fixes. Untrusted future clocks cannot extend freshness indefinitely; define receipt/capture age, skew and accuracy.

Cell movement creates ghosts or gaps. Canonical latest versions, versioned memberships and cleanup support conservative query covers followed by filtering/deduplication. Filters cannot recover candidates missed by insufficient covers. Warm failed shards from fresh authenticated reports/logs under policy and expose degraded coverage rather than counting stale positions as available.

7. Cells support proximity rather than road routing

Four steps from search coverage to assignment authority

  1. Cover

    Cover search bounds conservatively, including borders and unavailable shards.

  2. Verify candidates

    Join latest epochs/sequences/cells; check freshness/accuracy and remove ghosts.

  3. Rank by road ETA

    Bound routing work using coherent graph/profile/traffic context.

  4. Reserve / commit

    Authority rechecks trips, current offers and driver reservations at commit.

One step cannot replace the next step’s proof: fast or nearby does not establish assignability.

Spatial hierarchies reduce candidates while road ETAs account for bridges, direction, pickup restrictions and traffic. A hex ring is not automatically an exact radius cover; resolution, location, distortions and bounds matter. Use conservative covers, required exact filters and coherent road-time ranking.

H3 has resolutions zero through fifteen and twelve pentagons per resolution; areas vary geographically. Parent/child relationships are logically exact but geometrically approximate. Do not infer complete polygon containment from parent IDs. These distinctions affect pricing aggregates and boundary covers. S2, geohash or other indexes are valid with understood geometry and coverage.

Hash placement does not equalize geographic demand. Airports, stadiums and events remain hot, requiring measured splits, partitions, bounds and admission. Version ownership migrations; gossip liveness does not prove exclusive assignment authority.

8. Batch matching: objectives and collection delay

Greedy assigns free eligible drivers in arrival order. It need not minimize total ETA, while batching adds collection/compute delays. Two fixtures distinguish greedy regret from total-versus-worst trade-offs. Maximize cardinality first; fewer matches do not become better because their summed ETA is smaller. Track unmatched users separately.

Uniform arrivals in a T-window give roughly T/2 mean collection under that assumption; early arrivals wait nearly T. Forecast error, acceptance and reoffers alter actual pickups. Real constraints include capacity, products, accessibility, preferences, fairness, airports and finishing trips. Tiny exact enumeration teaches objectives rather than optimizing a city.

9. Commit trip and driver reservations under shared invariants

Two requested trips can each CAS successfully onto one driver. Reading availability outside the commit has a TOCTOU race. Correct transaction scope, isolation and constraints can guard trips, sessions/reservations, offers and results together. Cross-shard designs need durable reserve/confirm/cancel protocols with tokens, deadlines and recovery rather than an unexplained lock.

If an old D3 offer expires and D2 has a new one while the trip remains requested, state-only checks let D3 win incorrectly. Validate IDs, generations, server expiry, sessions and expected versions. UI countdowns are estimates; authority checks at commit. Lost replies replay retained acceptance rather than create new offers/charges.

BEGIN SERIALIZABLE
  result = lookup(principal_scope, command_id)

  else:

    check offer expiry using authoritative time semantics

    write trip assignment and matching driver reservation

# Trip-only CAS cannot prevent D1 taking both T1 and T2.
# Availability read before the transaction is a hint, not the final guard.
# Publish notifications only through committed intent and replay-safe delivery.

Pseudocode for a supported serializable scope; actual schemas/constraints must enforce both trip and driver uniqueness. Do not call external effects inside retryable callbacks.

10. ETAs and quotes are contextual predictions

Routing uses graph/profile and traffic age, with uncertainty by geography, pickup geometry and horizon. Residual models can improve baselines without one universal correction. Evaluate cohorts, quantiles, calibration and drift, correlating versions with outcomes. Slow models may fall back to routing under explicit policies.

Pricing uses fresh supply/demand, limits and expiry. A request/driver ratio is not a real surge formula; smoothing, lag, policies and incentives create feedback. GPS dots do not make busy/offline drivers eligible. Deduplicate/window events and preserve accepted quote terms or explicit requoting; client displays do not replace server authority.

11. Mobile, regional and replay recovery

Recover fast projection

  • Warm from authenticated reports/logs, expose coverage gaps and reject expired candidates.

Recover authority

  • Fence old writers and verify durable trips, reservations, results and versions.

Recover side effects

  • Replay outbox/CDC with economic identity and reconcile uncertain payments.

Clients keep observed versions, cursors and pending IDs. Reconnect replays bounded gaps or explicitly refreshes expired cursors. Protected capsules may aid context recovery but signatures/encryption do not prove freshness. Validate scope, version/expiry and durable authority with anti-rollback evidence. Phones cannot unilaterally declare completion or free drivers.

Failover needs explicit replication/RPO, fencing and capacity. Location warmup can be eventual while committed trip/payment results need distinct durability/reconciliation. Next pings do not justify discarding committed state. Drill disconnected phones, routing/connections, replay batches and duplicate billing protection.

12. Keep streams, ML and privacy under explicit boundaries

Business changes use outbox/CDC while raw GPS has separate acceptance/loss policy. Replication adds lag, checkpoints and duplicates; restarted consumers need retention-aware dedup and sink fencing. Global analytics cannot replace trip authority. Backfills/features must not charge, resend receipts or recreate deleted personal data.

Location history remains sensitive despite rotating IDs or aggregation. Minimize retention and govern purpose, access, exports, consent, deletion and sparse groups without claiming automatic anonymity. Temporal splits prevent leakage; lineage, freshness and release cohorts need reproducibility. Ranking never overrides eligibility authority.

13. Choose storage and service boundaries by invariants

Relational designs, custom/sharded stores and distributed transactional cores have workload-specific trade-offs. Append-only versions do not prevent conflicting changes. Distributed transactions can simplify coordination when schemas/locality support invariants, while latency, hotspots, dependencies and costs require real workload evidence. Transition rates differ from all database transactions.

Domain interfaces bound internals: fulfillment owns trips/sessions, marketplace matching/quotes, maps routing/ETA and payments money operations. Types/tests do not alone prove semantics, permission or deadline safety. Measure fan-out, change coupling and ownership, retaining modular applications while adequate. Large-scale choices are not small-team mandates.

15. Explain one trip end to end

Start with requirements/invariants, size the planes, trace acceptance, choose two deep dives and prove failures. State non-pooling assumptions; pooling/delivery change capacity and waypoint constraints. These answers test reasoning rather than prescribe one design for all products.

Does RAM fit imply one Redis node is enough?

No. Raw sizes omit indexing, allocation, replication, CPU/network, hotspots and resilience. Rebuildable projections do not justify losing committed trips. Choose placement and warmup under actual workload/SLO evidence.

Can two successful trip CAS operations assign one driver?

Yes: each trip protects itself. Atomically check/reserve driver capacity with assignment or use an equivalent protocol, retaining ownership versions and replay results. Pre-commit availability reads are insufficient.

Does one H3 ring always cover a radius?

No. Coverage varies with geometry, resolution, location, pentagons and radius. Use conservative covers and required exact filters. Road ETA differs from distance; logical hierarchy is not exact polygon containment.

Does batching always reduce every rider’s wait?

No. Total differs from worst/fairness and batching adds delay. The regret fixture reduces assignment sum from fifty to twenty-six seconds, but twenty seconds collection per rider yields sixty-six total. Track unmatched users, arrivals and acceptance/reoffers.

Does trip CAS reject an expired D3 offer arriving before D2?

Not while the trip remains requested. Validate current offer/generation, session, expected version and server expiry. State-only checks admit obsolete offers; committed retries return retained outcomes.

Does an encrypted phone capsule guarantee trip durability?

No. Phones may be offline or replay stale capsules; encryption alone is not integrity. Validate protected capsules against scope, versions, expiry and anti-rollback evidence under durable authority/RPO. They do not replace replication, backups and drills.

Can a good offline ETA model replace eligibility checks?

No. Predictions rank candidates while authority revalidates sessions, product eligibility and reservations. Evaluate cohorts, drift, latency and feature consistency; model fallbacks never bypass invariants.

Why does post-transaction payment still need an outbox?

Crashes between commit and publication can lose intent; publishing first can act on uncommitted state. Atomic intent and sink identities preserve recovery, external uncertainty and reconciliation. Asynchrony does not imply exactly-once.
