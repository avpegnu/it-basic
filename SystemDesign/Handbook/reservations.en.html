Designing a Reservation System: inventory, holds and late payments

Explore whole-stay races and expired-hold revival; connect inventory transactions with payment recovery, waiting-room admission and channel capacity boundaries.

From availability hints to confirmed entitlements

  1. Browse

    Read cached hints and trusted quotes/dates.

  2. Acquire

    Acquire whole-stay capacity, hold identities and deadlines.

  3. Confirm or release

    Apply verified payment/deadline/version checks and counters.

  4. Recover and scale

    Recover late payments and manage admission/shards/channels.

Inventory entitlements and payment states are linked workflows, not one network-spanning transaction.

  • Whole-stay inventory, reservations/results and outboxes share atomic boundaries.
  • Hold states need expiry/version semantics; payment timeouts do not prove no charge.
  • Late captures cannot revive released entitlements; recoveries/refunds have separate identities.
  • Conditional updates still abort/deadlock/have ambiguous commits; retries are bounded/idempotent.
  • Waiting rooms, TTLs and asynchronous channel events do not inherently provide durable global capacity guarantees.

1. Are you selling room types, seats or intervals?

Inventory unit

  • Hotel-local dates are calendar dates rather than always twenty-four-hour durations.

All-or-nothing stays

  • One missing night rolls back the whole stay rather than leaking partial allocations.

Money differs from entitlement

  • Late payment success cannot revive entitlements already reassigned.

The baseline sells quantities by hotel, room type and local date for half-open stays, with search/quotes, holds, payments, cancellations and administration. Rooms within types are assumed fungible; specific units, maintenance, adjacent seats or same-unit stays can require interval/assignment models. Room identity is not universally unnecessary.

Active holds plus confirmed commitments must not exceed integer sellable limits on any night/resource within the authority boundary. Baseline overbooking is zero; deliberate overbooking requires explicit limits/rounding/remediation policies. Stale search hints grant no entitlements. Sub-second booking p99 excluding payments is an illustrative benchmark target.

2. Low average booking rates do not eliminate contention

One million rooms at seventy-percent occupancy imply 700,000 occupied room-nights/day. Three-night steady-state stays suggest roughly 233,333 daily arrivals; equating bookings with arrivals adds a steady-flow assumption. That gives 2.70 average bookings/second, 270 views/second at an assumed hundredfold ratio and 2,700 at tenfold peaks.

Five thousand hotels times twenty types times 365 days times two years yield seventy-three million nightly rows before reservations/lines/indexes/outboxes. Last-room sales can serialize requests despite low cluster averages. Multi-night stays, payments, retries, expiry and cancellation add traffic; benchmark lock waits, aborts, durability and failure capacity separately.

3. Hold APIs use stable results and trusted quotes

Validate quantities, hotel-local dates, stay bounds, permissions and trusted quote prices/currencies/versions/expiries. Browsers cannot confirm by claiming payment success. Scope idempotency by guest/merchant/operation with dates/type/quantity/quote fingerprints; ambiguous commits replay existing reservation results rather than decrement inventory again.

          4. Cheap browsing and authoritative booking paths

          Search caches project availability/rates through TTLs/events. Stale-positive hints can legitimately lead to sold-out hold responses under clear contracts. Booking transactions inspect authoritative rows. Hotel shards hold inventory/reservations/idempotency/outboxes together, while external payment starts after commit without locks during user authentication/payment.

          Lagging replicas/caches cannot establish last-room decisions. Failovers/split-brain handling must preserve authoritative writes and acknowledged durability; row algorithms across independent primaries do not share the invariant. CDNs serve static photos rather than replace booking correctness boundaries.

          Browsing may be stale; inventory authorities grant entitlements

          Component map; the complete ordered request paths are explained below.

          Browse and quote

          1. Search local calendar dates, room types and quantities.
          2. Query availability/rates under policy.
          3. Read potentially stale versioned/as-of projections.
          4. Return quotes/hints to revalidate at hold creation.

          Acquire a whole-stay hold

          1. Post holds with guest/operation-scoped keys and quote identities.
          2. Validate authorized quantities/dates and trusted prices.
          3. One transaction deduplicates, locks expected nightly rows, checks capacity and records held units, hold lines, results and outboxes.
          4. Commit under booking durability; missing one night rolls the whole stay back.
          5. Return stable reservation identities/expiries without duplicate holds on retry.
          6. Start external payments after commit without retaining inventory-row locks.

          Verified payments and entitlement checks

          1. Verified outcomes bind correct reservation/payment operations and can arrive late.
          2. Validate locked active holds, versions, deadlines and lines; atomically confirm valid entitlements.
          3. Reject stale confirmations of released/reallocated holds while preserving payment recovery evidence.
          4. When needed, acquire new valid holds or use idempotent compensation/refund workflows rather than force-confirming.

          Sweep/release and refresh projections

          1. Claim due holds and conditionally release all lines under status/version/expiry checks in one transaction.
          2. Committed expiry/cancellation events pass through durable outboxes.
          3. Update projections idempotently/by version; booking truth does not wait for caches.

          Flash-sale admission before inventory

          1. Use authenticated queue/lottery admission and bot controls under fairness policies.
          2. Admit bounded concurrency with scoped short-lived replay-resistant tokens.
          3. Admission grants permission to attempt booking; inventory still decides.
          4. Keep durable conditional ownership/acquisition even after queue admission.
          Guest / client
          Queries dates/quotes, retains request keys and does not establish payment success itself.
          Authenticated API
          Validates guests/tenants, dates/quantities, quotes and any signed admission.
          Search / rates
          Read-heavy availability/price hints carry as-of times and quote expiries.
          Availability projection
          CDC/outbox refreshes can lag; cached availability does not guarantee successful holds.
          Admission / waiting room
          Optional flash-sale admission bounds concurrency but does not establish inventory ownership.
          Reservation service
          Uses short transactions for whole-stay holds and guarded confirmations/releases with separate payment/recovery identities.
          Inventory + reservations
          Stores preprovisioned nightly rows, integer limits, hold lines, state/version/expiry, idempotency and outboxes.
          Payment / recovery
          Provides verified capture/auth facts, unknown-outcome handling, same-operation retries and compensation/refund workflows.
          Expiry sweeper
          Claims expired holds and conditionally releases once with audits; TTL notifications are hints.
          Reservation events
          Committed outboxes feed cache/email/audit consumers; duplicate events do not release inventory again.

          5. Check and reserve whole stays in short transactions

          Reading availability before later writes creates races where both guests see one unit. Conditional updates, explicit locks or optimistic versions with correct retries can help, but capacity checks/mutations, hold lines, idempotency results and outboxes need one transaction. Checks constrain rows without joining separately committed stay fragments.

          Preprovision expected nightly rows or safely create/lock them. Validate exact calendar dates and night counts; missing rows are not free rooms. Use consistent lock ordering across creation/confirmation/cancellation/admin or handle deadlocks through bounded idempotent retries. Range updates do not promise date-order locks; conditional statements can still time out, deadlock, abort or have ambiguous commits.

          6. Example transactions validate all expected rows before mutations

          The example pessimistically locks one hotel/type. Applications must validate dates/capacity and roll back on failure; comments do not enforce logic. Hold deadlines use contract-defined trusted decision times after relevant locks rather than browser clocks. Transaction-start timestamps can age during waits, so choose time semantics deliberately.

          BEGIN;
SELECT day, sellable_limit, held, confirmed
FROM room_inventory
WHERE hotel_id = :hotel AND room_type_id = :type
  AND day >= :check_in AND day < :check_out
ORDER BY day
FOR UPDATE;
-- Application: require the exact expected calendar-date set.
-- Require held + confirmed + :qty <= sellable_limit on EVERY row.
-- Otherwise ROLLBACK; do not continue.
UPDATE room_inventory SET held = held + :qty
WHERE hotel_id = :hotel AND room_type_id = :type
  AND day >= :check_in AND day < :check_out;
-- In this SAME transaction: reservation + hold_lines + idempotency result + outbox.
-- All statements/constraints must succeed before COMMIT.
COMMIT;

          The SQL sketch requires checks, transaction handling and constraints; it is not a complete production endpoint.

          7. Hold expiry is an entitlement policy, not payment cancellation

          Holds reserve inventory until deadlines. Confirmation requires verified payment evidence, matching identities/versions, active status and deadline eligibility under authority time. HELD alone is insufficient when expired-but-unswept. Products may allow controlled extensions, but clients cannot unilaterally extend TTLs.

          Sweepers claim due rows and atomically transition statuses/versions, release lines and write outboxes. Confirmation/release serialize at shared state/inventory boundaries so only the winner changes counters. Redis expiry notifications can hint rather than provide exactly-once releases, and deadlines do not cancel external PSP calls.

          8. Unknown outcomes and late captures need payment recovery

          Timeouts are not failures. Policies may extend holds within bounds or expire them with late-payment recovery, trading utilization and experience. Provider success cannot force confirmation after release/reallocation. Reacquire valid capacity/new holds or use idempotent referenced refund/compensation workflows with owned unresolved queues.

          Authorization-before-capture can reduce some mismatches while retaining provider windows, failures and ambiguous outcomes. Sagas do not make refunds instantaneous/infallible. Link and audit booking entitlements, payment facts and recovery states rather than collapsing them into PAID.

          9. Cancel/release once while refunds have their own lifecycle

          Cancellations verify permissions/policies/versions. Transactions transition eligible holds/confirmations, adjust the correct counters and create refund intents/outboxes as needed. Duplicate requests replay results and duplicate events do not release inventory again. Already-expired reservations release nothing again.

          Cancelled reservations can still have processing/unknown refunds; freed inventory does not establish returned money. Recovery operations have separate identities/evidence. Amendments need atomic new-resource acquisition/old-resource release or explicit sagas; deleting old bookings before acquiring replacements does not provide atomic amendments.

          10. Inventory schemas store explicit integer limits

          Nightly rows store hotel/type/day, integer sellable limits, held/confirmed counts and optional versions, constrained nonnegative and within limits. Reservation lines preserve quantities/dates, quote snapshots, state/version/expiry and guests/tenants. Scoped identities/payment links need retry/recovery-compatible retention.

          A condition reserved < total×1.10 can allow two integer sales against total one and threshold 1.1. Materialize explicit integer limits under rounding policies. Reducing limits below commitments needs conflict/relocation/remediation workflows rather than bypassing constraints. Physical assignments/maintenance add constraints beyond fungibility.

          11. Flash-sale admission does not replace durable ownership

          Layers protecting ticket drops

          1. Admit

            Apply queue/lottery policies, authentication and signed tokens.

          2. Bound concurrency

            Bound locking/worker demand under measured capacity.

          3. Acquire ownership

            Acquire durable conditional holds or proven escrow ownership.

          4. Confirm and recover

            Handle payments/expiry/versions and owned compensation.

          Outer layers reduce demand while ownership remains correct under failure.

          Waiting rooms/lotteries bound booking requests using scoped expiring replay-resistant tokens, guest limits and bot controls. Admit according to measured locking/checkout capacity rather than arbitrary rates. FIFO, lotteries/priorities and reentry policies are product decisions. Admission grants attempts rather than seat ownership.

          In-memory counters/TTL holds followed by asynchronous SQL do not inherently prevent failover/stale-owner oversell. Strict guarantees need durable authorities/fencing or proven escrow allocations/transfer protocols. Explicitly provisional holds can precede durable commits but are not confirmations. Discovering oversell afterward does not prove it never happened.

          12. Shard by transactions while channels share capacity coherently

          Hotel sharding keeps single-hotel stays local. Multi-hotel carts, cross-property packages and cross-pool assignments require distributed protocols or explicit partial/cancellation semantics. Regional replicas serve browsing while inventory retains appropriate writable ownership/epochs.

          Sites/external channels sharing pools need authoritative reservations or bounded allocations/transfers. Eventual availability synchronization alone can oversell across stale channel views. Import bookings idempotently, define latency/conflict policies and reconcile, rather than treating asynchronous events as global capacity proofs.

          13. Measure lock waits, hold ages and paid-unconfirmed cases

          Measure hot hotel/type/date success/sold-out/conflicts, lock waits/deadlocks/aborts, ambiguous commits, hold/expiry ages, browse staleness and unknown/late-payment/refund ages. Correlate reservation/hold/payment identities without exposing secrets. Paid-unconfirmed cases require ownership/escalation rather than warning logs alone.

          14. Interview questions on inventory and expiry

          Draw nightly rows with held/confirmed/limit values, request identities and payment attempts. Check each transaction/crash state rather than merely proposing locks without resources or lifetimes.

          What happens when two guests book the last room?

          Atomically check/mutate capacity with holds/results/outboxes in short transactions. Serialize common rows, constrain commits within limits and handle abort/ambiguous retries idempotently.

          Does a three-night stay retain night one when night two is full?

          Not under whole-stay semantics. Validate calendar rows and roll back every mutation on missing capacity/rows. Split stays require explicit offers, not leaked allocations.

          Does status=HELD suffice for deadline-safe confirmation?

          Not for expired-unswept rows. Validate hold/version/deadline eligibility and payment identities within the authority boundary, with explicit extension policies.

          What if payment succeeds after release and another guest books?

          Do not force confirmation. Reacquire valid capacity or use identified recoverable compensation/refunds. Unknown outcomes are not failures and expiry does not cancel external calls.

          Do conditional updates eliminate every retry?

          No: timeouts, deadlocks, serialization aborts and ambiguous commits remain. Use bounded retries, stable identities, consistent locking and committed-result replay.

          Do Redis TTL holds plus asynchronous persistence guarantee no oversell?

          Not inherently. Failover, stale owners and lost holds need durable authorities/fencing or escrow protocols. Admission reduces load, and post-failure reconciliation does not replace safety.

          Does reserved < total×1.1 implement ten-percent overbooking correctly?

          Not for all integer capacities: a threshold of 1.1 can admit a second unit. Use explicitly rounded integer limits, commitment constraints and administrative conflict handling.

          Does channel availability synchronization prevent every double booking?

          Asynchronous stale synchronization is insufficient. Use central authorities or bounded channel allocations/transfers with idempotent identities and explicit conflict/latency/reconciliation policies.
