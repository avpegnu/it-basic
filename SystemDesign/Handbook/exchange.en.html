Designing a Stock Exchange: matching, committed replay and risk

1. Define the market model and its invariants

Matching correctness

  • Price priority, FIFO and quantity conservation under explicit rules.

Recovery correctness

  • Committed cuts, full state, deterministic replay and fenced outputs.

Risk correctness

  • Shared account budgets use atomic or conservative reservation lifecycles.

This educational continuous exchange accepts, cancels and replaces orders with per-symbol price-time priority, private execution reports and public market data. Residual limits rest as GTC orders; markets are IOC and expire unfilled quantities. Crossing orders execute at resting-maker prices in this baseline. These rules are not universal across venues or order types. Auctions, hidden/reserve/pegged orders, derivatives and complex margin need separate specifications.

Core invariants require ordered application of committed commands, no double execution of quantities, no orders created by rejection, stable execution identities and reservations within hard account budgets. Accepted does not mean filled. Disconnections after submission leave unknown outcomes until recovery; using a fresh business ID can accidentally create a second order.

2. Service budgets are not latency benchmarks

To separate average from peak, an illustrative workload of 100 million commands over 23,400 seconds averages about 4,274 commands/s and 12.8 GB of 128-byte input payloads. A 400K/s peak is nearly 94 times that average; sustaining it throughout the session is not a daily forecast. Distributions and burst durations guide queue/log/output reserves; the following numbers intentionally show a separate peak-sustained upper scenario.

For a sizing exercise, assume 400,000 peak commands/s, 128-byte mean payloads and a 6.5-hour session. Payload ingress is 51.2 MB/s; sustaining that peak throughout the session yields 9.36 billion commands and about 1.198 TB before framing/indexes/replicas. This is a conditional scenario rather than measured traffic or a log-size prediction. Cancels, replacements and rejections consume resources without trades; output work varies with fills and feed messages.

The reciprocal 2.5 μs is an average service-capacity budget for a serial owner handling the entire rate, not a promised end-to-end latency or proof that one core is sufficient. Near saturation, bursts and variable-cost commands cause queueing. Measure tails at defined network boundaries alongside queue depth/age, persistence, fills per command and recovery lag. Acceptance, trade publication and failover need separate objectives.

3. Separate wire sessions, business commands and engine indexes

Entry can use FIX session/application messages or binary order-entry protocols; administrative/query REST APIs are not the same hot-path wire contract. Native market data also differs from browser WebSocket relays. Bound decoding and validate integer ticks/lots, principals/accounts and session state so malformed inputs cannot stall book ownership.

Session sequences support session recovery, scoped client IDs support business deduplication and engine indexes order a shard. They are not interchangeable. Reusing an ID with different payloads must conflict. Results and dedup scope/retention must survive reconnects/failovers; short gateway caches are insufficient after ambiguous responses. Define receipt acknowledgements, durable acceptance and execution reports separately.

          4. State model: identity, ownership and recovery cuts

          Do not add per-fill hot-path SQL transactions merely to fit a table design. In-memory books can use authoritative logs/snapshots with relational projections for queries/audits under freshness contracts. Records need mutation ownership, identity-reuse rules and recovery cuts. Risk/post-trade effects share business identities without implicitly sharing database transactions with matching.

          5. Separate entry, committed state and projections

          Gateways own sessions/authentication/admission, risk authorities own shared budgets, logs establish committed order and a single owner applies matching state. Outputs drive private reports, market data and post-trade projections. Keep query databases/cold audits outside the matching loop. Acknowledgement/publication boundaries must match recovery guarantees: exposed decisions promised to survive a failure model must first be recoverably committed.

          The diagram uses pre-commit reservations as one orchestration baseline. Crashes can orphan reservations and reserve timeouts can be ambiguous. Stable identities, reconciliation and release/cancel protocols are required; never auto-expire funds while orders can still execute. Account-local risk or shard escrow are alternatives, but they must prove no double-spending or premature release rather than merely relocate boxes.

          One committed order with replayable outputs

          Component map; the complete ordered request paths are explained below.

          Entry, risk reservation, log commitment and output authority are separate boundaries. The book consumes committed inputs; market-data and post-trade projections retain their own output identities.

          New orders and executions

          1. Send command identities and order fields over authenticated sessions.
          2. Reserve conservative account budgets under stable reservation IDs.
          3. Log commands and authoritative reservation evidence under the chosen ordering contract.
          4. Apply committed inputs in index order.
          5. Produce outputs identified by log index/ordinal and retain command results.
          6. Return private acceptance/rejection/execution reports under recovery contracts.
          7. Clients distinguish accepted orders from completed executions.

          Public feeds and gaps

          1. Produce permitted public-book projections without account secrets.
          2. Publishers consume stable outputs and maintain feed/session sequencing.
          3. Publish channel deltas; consumers detect missing intervals.
          4. Request retransmission or a consistent snapshot at barrier S.
          5. Recover with snapshot S and contiguous later deltas before marking live.

          Crash, replay and fenced promotion

          1. Replicate committed inputs and pin snapshot indexes/configuration.
          2. Establish committed cuts, replay suffixes and exclude uncommitted assignments.
          3. Fence old epochs at output boundaries before enabling new publishers.
          4. Restore orders, dedup/control state and pinned rules.
          5. Reproduce stable identities; sinks deduplicate outputs received before crashes.

          Close risk reservations

          1. Committed cancels/rejections/fills determine lifecycle transitions.
          2. Apply identified consumption/releases without double-returning credit.
          3. Durably project executions and reconcile accounting state.

          Ordered trading halts

          1. Log authorized halts/resumes/rule changes as control inputs.
          2. Change trading modes at exact committed boundaries.
          3. Emit mode events under explicit cancel-during-halt policies.

          6. Price levels, FIFO and partial fills

          Use ordered price levels and FIFO queues per level with an order-ID locator for cancellation. Select the best opposing price, test crossing, execute the minimum remaining quantity and update/remove makers until exhausted or ineligible. GTC remainders rest; IOC remainders expire. Trees can provide O(log P) level operations and indexed linked queues O(1) node cancellation, subject to correct locators and reclamation.

          Use bounded integer ticks/lots and guard notional/reservation overflow. Aggressive orders may sweep many levels, so work is not always O(1). Under accepted-order semantics, incoming quantity equals fills plus rested plus expired quantities; retries must not add fills. The toy model uses arrays rather than production indexes for inspectability.

          7. Cancel, reduce and replace have explicit races and policies

          Cancellation cannot reverse executions earlier in committed order. If fills win the race, cancellation sees less remaining quantity or absence; if cancellation wins, later aggressors cannot match the withdrawn order. Gateway receipt is not cancellation completion. This lab preserves priority for reductions but replaces orders with fresh identities/priorities even at unchanged prices. Real protocols distinguish operations; not every quantity reduction through replacement preserves priority.

          Define self-trade prevention, minimum quantities, price bands, lot changes and the fate of old orders after rejected replacements. These can change outcomes relative to the toy model. Pin rule versions and log transitions instead of reading volatile configuration during replay. Multi-action commands also need atomicity and output ordering; one batch sequence number does not solve partial failures.

          8. Assigned, committed, applied and published are separate cuts

          Label every boundary

          1. Assigned

            Which indexes were allocated?

          2. Committed

            Which inputs must survive the stated failures?

          3. Applied

            How far has the book applied contiguously?

          4. Published

            Which outputs have consumers received/committed?

          Each cut answers a different question.

          Assigned indexes order commands but may still exist only in volatile memory. Local durable journals and replicated committed logs cover different failures; memory maps or replica RAM do not imply power-loss durability. This baseline applies and exposes authoritative results from committed prefixes, accepting that latency cost. Speculative application requires strict separation of speculative state/outputs from irreversible results.

          A timeout after commitment but before a reply is ambiguous. Recover results under the original ID; duplicate log inputs can return retained results without new trades. Publication has its own windows: committed-but-unsent outputs need replay, while sent-but-uncheckpointed outputs need downstream deduplication. Exactly-once business effects require boundary-spanning protocols rather than a broker flag.

          9. Deterministic replay needs more than the order book

          Identical inputs imply identical states only with deterministic code, rules/configuration, arithmetic, iteration and external decisions. Timers, randomness, controls, reference prices, risk decisions and administrative actions need logged evidence or versioned deterministic derivations. Replay must not query current clocks or risk decisions. Audit timestamps are not implicit tie-breakers when the rules use sequencer order.

          Snapshots need exact indexes, schemas/configurations and integrity. Capture orders/queues, FIFO-preserving locator reconstruction, retained results, control state and output progress, coordinating reservations/positions across owners. Restore and replay contiguous suffixes without skipping gaps. Shadow comparisons expose divergence; checksums only detect differences in covered state, not correctness of the underlying rules.

          Promote with evidence rather than health alone

          1. Fence the old writer

            Obtain new authority and enforce fencing at entry/output sinks.

          2. Establish the committed cut

            Uncommitted assignment #14 is not an accepted effect.

          3. Restore and replay

            Restore orders, FIFO, deduplication, rules, controls and contiguous replay through #13.

          4. Recover outputs before resuming

            Preserve fill IDs, deduplicate sinks and recover session/feed cursors.

          Readiness and exclusive authority are both required before publication; detailed ordering depends on the chosen ownership protocol.

          Catch-up does not authorize a standby to accept new work. External ownership/epochs require enforcement at suitable entry/output boundaries or sinks because partitioned old leaders may remain alive. Uncertain committed cuts or authority require the designed safe halt rather than divergent publishers. Failover drills must verify reports, feed sequences and post-trade effects, not just process health.

          Execution identities must survive leadership changes: for example shard/log-session identity, committed index and output ordinal. New epochs fence writers; making epochs fresh business identities can defeat deduplication. Log-session identity must also prevent reuse across resets/archives. The lab omits it only because it has one fixed toy log, not because index/ordinal is universally sufficient.

          11. Shared account risk crosses symbol ownership

          One account can submit to two symbol shards concurrently. Two stale successful prechecks do not prove valid aggregate reservations. Account authorities, conservative shard escrow or partial colocation trade latency, utilization and coordination costs. Reservations require stable identities, bounded amounts and reserve/consume/release transitions with deduplication and ambiguous-outcome recovery.

          Gateway timeouts, disconnections or TTL expiry do not justify releasing funds while orders remain executable. Partial fills convert reserved to executed exposure; cancels/rejections release the proper remainder. Fees, price bands and market-order bounds require separate risk rules. The lab has no executed exposure, so conservation is limited to available plus reserved budget; production invariants are broader. Reconciliation and kill switches complement rather than replace hard pre-trade enforcement.

          12. Feed gaps, snapshots and fairness have explicit contracts

          Public feeds may expose orders or aggregated levels without private account identities. Transport/session sequencing detects gaps, duplicates and restarts. Missing delta N prevents treating N+1 as complete state: use bounded buffering/retransmission or mark stale and obtain a snapshot at barrier S. Discard deltas through S and apply contiguous later events before marking live. Insufficient retention/buffers require renewed recovery rather than silently skipping missing intervals.

          Native binary feeds can use multicast/UDP or sequenced TCP transports; browser WebSocket relays are distinct projections with their own buffers/backpressure. Sequencers establish ordering policies without removing every network advantage, and multicast does not deliver simultaneously everywhere. Discuss fairness through defined ingress policies, timestamps, observable latency and access rules rather than absolute claims based on single-threaded matching.

          13. Optimize the hot path while preserving semantics

          Network boundary

          • Colocation reduces propagation distance; ingress scheduling and fairness still need definitions and measurements.
          • Kernel bypass or NIC offload can reduce jitter for suitable workloads at additional complexity and observability cost.

          CPU / memory boundary

          • Core affinity, locality and preallocation limit migration and allocation without eliminating every cache miss.
          • Bounded ring buffers need publication barriers and slow-consumer policies; busy-waiting trades CPU for latency.

          Durability boundary

          • Batched journal flushing/replication must match acknowledgement contracts; mmap alone is not durable commitment.
          • Slow consumers need bounded backlogs/recovery; do not make critical outputs best-effort merely to conceal overload.

          A single owner avoids concurrent book mutation but does not remove allocations, GC, blocking I/O or cache misses. Use bounded preallocation, locality, integer arithmetic and explicit backpressure while keeping remote blocking calls outside matching. Ring buffers require correct publication/memory visibility, capacities and slow-consumer policies; writing arrays and incrementing indexes is not automatically a concurrent queue.

          Independent symbols can be sharded, but hot books retain serial bottlenecks. Price-range partitioning complicates atomic sweeps, best prices and FIFO. Global controls, shared risk and multi-leg orders still coordinate. Rebalancing needs fenced ownership, exact state/index transfer and generation-aware routing rather than stateless request migration.

          14. Auctions, halts and corrections need first-class rules

          Opening/closing auctions choose clearing prices under volume/imbalance/tie-breaking rules rather than simply run continuous matching faster. Interruptions, market-wide controls, self-trade prevention and instrument lifecycle need authorized sequenced inputs. Define accepted/cancelled/modified operations in each mode and transitions into auctions/resumption. The lab only models continuous/halted states, not auction clearing.

          Trade corrections/busts must not silently rewrite history or reuse identities ambiguously. Use authorized linked correction events and reconcile private/public/post-trade projections. Matching is not the entirety of clearing/settlement, whose positions/ledgers require durable idempotent processing and evidence. Legal and venue-specific correction decisions are outside this engineering model.

          15. Verify replay and failure boundaries with evidence

          Measure rejection reasons, assignment/commit/application/output lag, queue ages, command/fill rates, latency tails, reservation ages, feed gaps and replay divergence. Slow analytics/feed consumers must not block matching indefinitely; distinguish durable critical backlogs from best-effort telemetry. Admission must make nonacceptance explicit: dropping acknowledged commands without retained results is a correctness failure.

          Properties cover quantity conservation, priority, cancellation ownership, retries/conflicts, integer bounds and replay equality at snapshot cuts. Inject commit/send crashes, leader partitions, corrupt snapshots and duplicate releases, comparing all projections under stable identities. Benchmarks must state hardware, mixes, amplification, durability and percentiles; animations are not throughput evidence.

          16. Practice decisions with worked questions

          Draw a crossing order with two fills, a crash after sending outputs and an unfenced old leader. Identify committed inputs, once-counted executions, reservations and consumer recovery, then change a failure boundary and explain the trade-off.

          Does 400K commands/s prove 2.5 μs latency?

          No. It is a conditional average serial capacity budget. Networks, risk/commitment, queueing, variable fills and fan-out need separate measurement, boundaries, workload definitions and headroom.

          Does single-threading automatically make replay deterministic?

          No: clocks, randomness, configuration and unrecorded asynchronous decisions can diverge. Pin rules/arithmetic/iteration and record relevant inputs, including deduplication/control/risk boundaries.

          Does cancel receipt guarantee no further fills?

          No. Effects occur in committed application order; earlier fills remain valid. Wait for authoritative reports rather than equating gateway receipt with cancellation.

          Is a book snapshot enough for client retries?

          Not if results/IDs, rules/control state or output progress are absent. Retries can add executions despite matching initial levels. Test complete restoration at every cut under stable identities.

          Can matching standby state publish immediately?

          Only with committed readiness and externally enforced authority/fencing. Old leaders may still publish; preserve business execution identities across epochs to deduplicate effects.

          Do independent symbol prechecks enforce account-wide limits?

          No: both can observe 1,000 and accept 700+600. Atomic reservations or conservative escrow need reconciliation and deduplicated releases. Later detection does not retroactively enforce hard pre-trade limits.

          Can consumers skip missing deltas and continue?

          Not for correct books. Mark stale, recover intervals or barrier snapshots, then apply contiguous suffixes. Overflow/missing retention requires recovery restarts rather than pretending the latest price repairs all state.

          Why can same-price replacement lose FIFO?

          Priority is operation-specific. Lab replacement always renews priority while separate reductions preserve it. Do not generalize quantity-decrease behavior across all replacement messages; version the exact semantics.
