1. Nearby Friends needs authorized audiences as well as radii

Audience authorization

  • Check friendships, visibility, blocks and precision before disclosure.

Freshness

  • Observation times, accepted versions and explicit expiries serve different purposes.

Recovery

  • Live buses accelerate; snapshots and policy authorities repair missed updates.

The query asks which friends permitting this viewer are nearby, unlike discovering arbitrary restaurants or strangers. Friendships provide a relatively small candidate set while positions move. The baseline covers opt-in, visibility subsets, blocks/unfriending, radii, initial snapshots and live updates. Navigation and location history are outside scope. Decide whether disclosure includes precise coordinates, coarse positions or distance bands; UI blurring does not satisfy coarse disclosure after precise data was sent.

Dot freshness may be eventual over tens of seconds under product policy, but permission revocation needs a separate contract. Acceptable positional staleness does not justify ignoring blocks or disabled sharing. Specify effective times, partition behavior and the inability to recall received data. Offline clients should expose stale, unknown or expired state rather than ageless dots.

2. Logical fan-out can greatly exceed input

Assume a hundred million feature users with ten percent concurrently active: ten million publishers. Thirty-second updates yield roughly 333 thousand updates/second. Four hundred friends with ten percent online and authorized imply forty logical recipients per update, or 13.3 million delivery/filter actions per second before multiple devices. These assumed averages omit correlated activity and hot-user tails.

Gateways hosting multiple viewers of one publisher can share broker subscriptions and distribute locally, so broker copies need not equal logical recipients. Local permission/distance work and socket bytes remain. Server sizing requires measurements of connection memory, subscription state, TLS, CPU, networks and slow-consumer queues. A connections-per-server number needs a specific workload/limit benchmark rather than being a WebSocket default.

3. Sharing, snapshot and stream contracts

Clients must not subscribe to arbitrary user channels merely by knowing names. Servers authenticate principals, enforce appropriate WebSocket Origin/subprotocol/limits and construct authorized subscriptions. Location frames carry active writer/session epochs and monotonic sequences retained on retries. Observation, acceptance and expiry times differ; bounded client clock skew does not define global order.

4. Latest state and policy provide authority

Accepted locations conditionally update latest state before emitting live hints. Lost hints after writes remain recoverable through snapshots/reconciliation. Durable propagation may use appropriate outbox/log/CDC protocols; cache writes plus publishes are not inherently atomic. Positional and policy staleness cannot be treated identically without conditions: disclosures enforce authorization contracts, while dots stay within freshness windows.

Friend audiences, policy authority and latest-state recovery

Component map; the complete ordered request paths are explained below.

Accept a new fix

  1. Send stable writer/session epochs and sequences with bounded accuracy/times.
  2. Validate active writers and consent under authority contracts.
  3. Conditionally update latest state without stale session/sequence overwrites.
  4. Publish a hint after acceptance; reconciliation recovers lost hints.

Deliver to the friend audience

  1. Send one copy per interested gateway when multiplexing; broker delivery grants no permission.
  2. Enforce current audience/disclosure generations, precision and fail-closed uncertainty.
  3. Send versioned positions/distances or radius-exit removals with bounded coalescing.

The viewer moves

  1. Viewer movement changes the reference point for all candidates.
  2. Read/reuse sufficient authorized fresh candidates; pruning needs gap-free discovery policies.
  3. Recompute entries/exits even without new friend messages.

Revoke sharing

  1. Send a sharing-off/block command with expected generation and retry identity.
  2. Commit a consent epoch and define barrier/lease semantics before ACK.
  3. Propagate durable changes to gateways, state cleanup and reconciliation.
  4. Invalidate old permits and reject delayed frames; lost notices do not renew disclosure rights.
  5. Removal/invalidation cleans displays but cannot fully recall received data.

Reconnect or lose the last hint

  1. Reconnect with jitter and rebuild authorized subscriptions by generation.
  2. Coordinate subscription/buffering and snapshot boundaries; reconcile periodically without replay.
  3. Read current friendships, visibility and blocks.
  4. Read authorized fresh latest versions, including removals/absence.
  5. Reconcile snapshot generations without resurrecting dots from delayed events.

5. Count broker copies separately from local work

Per-publisher channels fit known friendship audiences. Gateways can reference-count subscriptions shared by local viewer sessions, unsubscribing when the last authorized viewer leaves. Do not broadcast payloads indiscriminately across sockets: filter authorization, precision, freshness and radius per viewer. Cache/fan-out designs need tenant/environment scope and suitable broker ACLs.

The lab uses exact placement for one synthetic audience. More devices increase logical deliveries; fewer distinct gateways reduce broker copies without removing local work. Global rates extrapolate the same distribution to ten million publishers, excluding protocol bytes, authorization lookups, distance costs, skew and coalescing. Measure these before real capacity planning.

6. Recompute when either participant moves

Receiver-side filtering must not run only on friend messages. Viewer movement or radius changes can affect every candidate even when friends stay still. Recompute entries/exits from cached authorized fresh positions or fetch sufficient candidates. Retaining only currently nearby people can miss formerly distant friends becoming close. Real geometry needs coordinate-appropriate distances; the lab uses planar km.

GPS noise near boundaries can cause repeated entries/exits. Accuracy-aware bands, dwell times or hysteresis need explicit semantics and must respect precision policies. Dots can become stale through either participant’s motion, expiry or revocation; removal reasons/version namespaces should prevent delayed nearby frames from resurrecting excluded state.

7. Timestamps do not replace writer or consent ordering

Device clocks can skew, restart or jump forward; timestamp-only ordering can suppress all legitimate updates after a future timestamp. Use server-authorized writer/session generations and monotonic sequences within generations; large sequences cannot override revoked consent. Multiple devices need an explicit active-writer or deterministic freshness/quality merge policy. A delayed old phone must not regain writer authority through a large sequence.

Observation times express age/quality, acceptance times server processing and expiry times disclosure/display deadlines. Bound fix age at ingestion rather than making old observations fresh merely because they arrived recently. Latest-state CAS and outgoing order need compatible contracts; snapshot replacement/removal generations prevent stale responses from overwriting new state.

8. Close the subscription–snapshot gap

Reading a snapshot before subscribing can lose intervening updates. Subscribe/buffer first, read latest versions and merge with guards; strict point-in-time cuts need suitable barrier/replay protocols. A lossy live bus may still lose later updates, so periodic authorized reconciliation belongs in the contract. Losing a final update differs from losing an intermediate one because another publication is not guaranteed.

Complete snapshots may remove no-longer-visible friends, while partial responses need completeness/warnings to avoid treating backend outages as authoritative removals. Reconnects rebuild current friendship/visibility state rather than reuse old subscriptions blindly. Jitter, backoff, bounded snapshot batches and admission controls prevent gateway failures from causing reconnect herds.

9. Revocation and expiry solve different problems

Disabling sharing commits a new policy generation and stops future disclosures under the stated contract. Best-effort removal events aid display cleanup but can be lost. Stale gateway permission caches require invalidation, bounded authorization leases or revocation barriers; uncertain fresh disclosures fail closed. Globally instantaneous revocation cannot be promised while stale permissions remain usable across partitions.

Backend key TTLs do not remove dots already delivered to clients. Use explicit expiries, active local timers, resume checks and periodic snapshot repair. Storage expiry notifications accelerate rather than guarantee deadline delivery. Even perfect protocols cannot recall screenshots or knowledge already received. The lab separates current policies, new forwards and previously received copies.

10. Stateful tiers need bounded draining and resubscription

Gateway budget

  • Connections, candidates, CPU, TLS and outbound bytes all impose limits.

Broker budget

  • Measure distinct gateway subscribers, hot channels and migration churn.

Incident controls

  • Longer intervals/coalescing trade freshness; retain revocation priority and recovery.

Gateways hold connections, subscriptions, candidates and send queues. Adding machines does not move live sockets automatically. Deployments stop admission, drain or reconnect with jitter, restore snapshots and limit rates. Coalesce latest positions for slow viewers or disconnect/resynchronize instead of growing queues indefinitely; policy removals must not be indefinitely lost behind location frames.

Classic Redis and sharded Pub/Sub propagate differently. Sharded channels use hash-slot routing with SPUBLISH/SSUBSCRIBE, not necessarily an application ring stored in etcd. Client-side sharding is another choice with separate migration/subscription ownership. Failovers and resharding create gaps requiring snapshot recovery. Kafka need not use a topic per user: keyed users can share partitioned topics when durable replay is needed, while viewer fan-out still requires design.

11. Battery, precision and background limits are distinct

Thirty-second GPS is a workload assumption, not an OS promise of scheduled background fixes. Permissions, app lifecycle, power modes and platform limits can delay or stop updates. Sampling intervals, batching delays and precision are different controls. Adaptive movement updates or significant-change APIs may reduce work for some workloads without guaranteeing coarse precision or fixed battery savings.

Interfaces should expose active sharing audiences, freshness and stop controls. Latest nearby state does not require long-lived trajectories. Coarsening disclosures limits precision without guaranteeing anonymity; repeated distance/radius queries can enable triangulation, requiring query/rate/privacy policies. Logs, analytics and administrative access also need retention/deletion controls.

12. Choose spatial indexes by access patterns

Friend-key fan-out suits small known candidate sets requiring push. Spatial indexes can also serve moving points depending on write rates, queries and engines; moving friends do not categorically rule them out. Geographic discovery matters more for strangers while authorization/safety remain separate. Cell subscriptions need conservative radius coverage and exact filtering rather than a universal eight-neighbor rule.

Cross-region friends may use publisher/home-region relays and regional viewer gateways. Skipping currently distant friends saves work but needs coarse discovery or periodic rechecks to detect approaches, otherwise false negatives persist. Multi-region policy revocation requires contracts distinct from positional freshness. Regional failover preserves fail-closed permissions rather than moving sockets to arbitrary cached regions.

13. Light storage does not make recovery free

Persist friendships, blocks, visibility and consent epochs, while retaining minimal latest-location state. If history is needed, justify its purpose/retention and time-bucket partitions to avoid unbounded per-user growth. Versioned deletions/tombstones or policy authorities must prevent delayed resurrection. TTLs do not erase every backup/log at the same instant.

Track input rates, logical checks, broker copies, socket bytes, drops/coalescing, freshness p99 and policy-enforcement lag. Do not audit privacy by indiscriminately logging precise disclosures; use controlled, retained identifiers/epochs. Drill lost final updates, missed revocations, expiries without events, viewer movement, gateway restarts and authority partitions.

14. Questions about fan-out, privacy and recovery

Separate authorization, new disclosures and old client copies. Draw a lost final message rather than assume another update will always repair failures.

Do forty friends always require forty broker copies?

These are logical recipients before multiple devices. Multiplexing reduces copies when viewers share gateways, while local filtering/socket work remains. Measure placements, bytes and skew rather than size from connections alone.

Must viewer movement recompute distances if friends stay still?

Yes. A changing reference point causes entries/exits. Publisher-event-only filtering retains stale dots and misses entries; pruning needs discovery/recheck mechanisms.

Does backend TTL automatically remove a client dot?

No. Dots are delivered copies, and expiry notifications may be delayed or lost. Use explicit expiries, timers/resume checks and authorized reconciliation. Key absence is not itself a client event.

Does a removal event enforce stopped sharing?

No. Authority commits and outgoing disclosures need invalidation/barrier/lease contracts with fail-closed uncertainty. Removals aid cleanup and cannot erase screenshots or received data.

Are client timestamps sufficient to reject old fixes?

Not with skew, restarts or multiple devices. Use authorized writer/session epochs and monotonic sequences, separate policy epochs and freshness bounds. Old devices do not win through larger clocks/sequences.

Can a lost update always wait for the next thirty-second update?

Another update may never arrive after a final fix or revocation. Snapshots and periodic reconciliation must repair final-state loss, while reconnects need current authorization.

Should all distant or cross-region subscriptions be discarded?

Only with discovery/rechecks preventing missed approaches. Use conservative coverage and distinguish regional authorization from position freshness. This is a measured trade-off rather than a universal shortcut.

Do significant-change APIs guarantee precision, freshness and low power together?

No. Permissions, lifecycle and workloads affect delivery, while intervals, precision and batching differ. Expose age/unknown states and request only necessary precision/background access.
