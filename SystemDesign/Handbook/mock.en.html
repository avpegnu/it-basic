Practice 36 prompts over 30, 45 or 60 minutes with six note areas, nonrepeating challenges and authoritative lesson summaries. Pause and restore, reflect with evidence and retain local history without predicting hiring levels.

1. What does a mock practice?

Construct an answer

  • Explain before looking and state uncertainty.

Adapt

  • Change assumptions or failures and reassess decisions.

Reflect

  • Use artifacts and examples rather than confidence-based levels.

Reading a solution differs from leading a discussion. Practice scoping, assumptions, units, choices and recovery, alone or with a partner or recording. The application neither records audio nor submits notes for grading.

Choose a specific goal such as effect deduplication, pacing or operation-level consistency. You need not cover every technology. Prompt selection prepares a session; explicit start begins timing.

2. Run a practice session

The deck covers 32 system-design and AI problem types plus frontend, Kubernetes, tenant API and reporting. Difficulty labels guide practice rather than standardized grading. Each prompt has three challenges and references its maintained lesson or topic.

Choose thirty, forty-five or sixty minutes; six phases scale to the exact budget. Selecting a note area does not change clock progress. Reference use is recorded as context rather than penalized.

Phase coaching

Clarify scope

Define actors, operations, invariants, non-goals and consequential unknowns.

  • Separate functional behavior from latency, availability and data safety.
  • State missing measurements as assumptions and identify decision-changing questions.
  • Choose a normal operation and an unacceptable outcome.

Estimate to guide decisions

Estimate quantities affecting capacity, partitioning or cost.

  • State units, population, windows and peak or skew assumptions.
  • Account for fan-out, retries, retention and headroom where relevant.
  • End each estimate with an implication and a validation method.

APIs, state and data

Define identities, access patterns and state transitions before storage details.

  • Describe requests, responses and pending, failed or repeated outcomes.
  • Identify authority and transaction or uniqueness scope.
  • State partition or ordering keys and cross-boundary costs.

End-to-end architecture

Walk reads and writes through clearly owned boundaries.

  • Add components only for a responsibility or state boundary.
  • Mark acknowledgement, commit, visibility and asynchronous handoffs.
  • Check alignment with requirements before diving deeper.

Deep dives and failures

Choose a risky boundary, explain enforcement and test a failure interleaving.

  • Compare feasible options using decisive criteria.
  • Try post-effect timeout, duplication, stale authority or changed access.
  • State degradation, recovery evidence and reversal conditions.

Wrap up and next steps

Close with decisions, risks and next validation steps.

  • Summarize paths and boundary choices.
  • State remaining risks, rollout, recovery and coordination owners.
  • Choose one concrete improvement for the next session.

3. Understand timing and checkpoints

The default forty-five minutes allocate five to scope, four to estimates, six to data, ten to architecture, fifteen to depth and five to closing. This is practice guidance rather than an interview rule. Other budgets scale proportionally and may include seconds.

The clock uses elapsed time rather than callback counts, avoiding simple throttling drift. Sleep behavior varies by platform; this is not a guaranteed examination timer. Pausing freezes time, while reloads and imports restore the latest checkpoint paused without charging offline time.

Notes use debounced saves, periodic checkpoints and permitted page-hide handling. Crashes, quotas or storage restrictions can leave recent edits outside the last checkpoint. Expiry stops timing without deleting notes or declaring the task complete.

5. Adapt to challenges for explicit reasons

Change a constraint and reassess reasoning

  1. Restate

    State the constraint and assumptions.

  2. Locate

    Locate affected quantities or invariants.

  3. Compare

    Compare fixes, costs and semantic changes.

  4. Validate

    Name validation and remaining valid decisions.

Keep explicit contracts after the change.

Challenges do not repeat within a session. Restate the constraint, recompute relevant quantities, identify broken boundaries and compare changes. Preserve what remains valid, but redesign when the new constraint genuinely requires it.

Post-effect timeouts need more than arbitrary retries, and region loss needs more than routing when authority is unsafe. Reuse invariant and ownership reasoning rather than memorizing extra components.

6. Use references without treating them as the only answer

References reuse current lesson summaries and open full articles separately. They neither know your assumptions nor review your work, and examples do not fit every workload. Reference use contextualizes practice rather than grading it.

When stuck, walk a request, revisit requirements and compare alternatives. If the concept is missing, study it and return. Learning with references is valid; do not pretend it is closed-book practice.

7. Review evidence without inferring a level

An evidenced example

  • I distinguished unknown outcomes from retryable failures and checked a post-acceptance crash.

A concrete omission

  • I proposed database fallback without sizing failure load or concurrency.

Next exercise

  • Revisit cache loss with bounded admission and freshness, then request feedback.

Review scope, numbers, design, depth, trade-offs, operations and communication using explicit states and notes. Give concrete examples or omissions and next steps. Evidence remains self-reported rather than independently verified.

Do not sum dimensions into hiring percentages or titles. Tasks, reference use and feedback differ, preventing standardized comparison. Ask reviewers for claim, diagram and test evidence rather than only numbers.

8. Retain notes and history deliberately

Mock uses separate storage. Notes stay per session and phase; starting another prompt archives the current session even if unfinished. Saving under one identity updates that history entry. Keep long-term exports beyond the twenty-session limit.

Imports validate bounds and show a preflight before replacing only Mock data. Historical text survives missing references. Detected corruption or external changes pause saving for export, reload or deliberate replacement.

Local storage is neither a backup nor a cross-tab transaction system. Use one writing tab; not every simultaneous race can be prevented. Browser cleanup or quotas can remove persistence. Exports include your notes and should be stored or shared deliberately.

9. Choose the next session from an actual gap

Choose one or two checkable improvements. Rework estimates, failure interleavings or recorded explanations as appropriate. Evidence of change is more useful than a self-score trend.

Changing difficulty labels is not progress by itself. Vary workloads, privacy, durability or costs to test transfer, and revisit concepts before another full mock.

10. Interview drill: assess the practice process

Explain how you know what improved.

Why does the app avoid level verdicts from self-ratings?

Self-reports are uncalibrated and tasks and assistance vary. Statuses do not establish independently verified competence; artifacts and contextual feedback are required.

Must a reference design be followed?

No. References illustrate reasoning under lesson assumptions. Choose alternatives when justified and validated against your actual requirements.

Does a reload charge offline time to the session?

No. Mock restores the saved elapsed checkpoint paused and requires resumption. Recent changes can precede a checkpoint, so retain exports and check save status.

Must most architecture remain unchanged after a challenge?

There is no absolute rule. Change what the new contract requires and explain affected assumptions and costs, neither reflexively rewriting nor preserving invalid choices.
