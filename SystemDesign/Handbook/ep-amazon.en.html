Building Checkout: from cart intent to stock, payments and shipments

Eleven stages, quote/order/failure traces and three labs explore cart merges, inventory expiry/late authorization and shuffle isolation under explicit authority/recovery boundaries.

  3. Order

No automatic giant transaction spans banks and warehouses.

1. Each domain needs its own consistency

Browse / cart

  • Bound stale displays and conflicting intent; never charge directly from cached prices.

Inventory / order

  • Recover quantities, ownership, state versions and accepted results.

Money / fulfillment

  • External outcomes can be uncertain or irreversible; track separate identities.

A store has no single consistency knob. Catalogs tolerate bounded staleness; carts merge edits; inventory allocates goods; payments own external money evidence; orders retain contracts and progress. This lesson builds an Amazon-style checkout through eleven pressures, not a current company deployment inventory.

Labs assume hard stock without backorders. Allowing backorders changes invariants and UX deliberately. Cart items are not stock holds, queue tokens are not order confirmations, and authorization is not capture/settlement. These distinctions matter more than database names.

2. Define what Place order promises

Intent differs from commitment

  1. Requote

    Retain immutable approved totals.

  2. Reserve

    Acquire current order-specific holds.

  3. Authorize

    Resolve accepted, declined or unknown evidence.

  4. Confirm

    Commit state and durable next-step intent.

Each arrow requires persisted transitions and recovery, not merely sequential calls.

Requests carry immutable cart/quote versions, delivery choices and stable checkout identities. Revalidate totals/currency at authority and obtain consent for material changes instead of silently charging another amount. Retained request hashes reject identity reuse with changed carts.

Distinguish received, payment pending, confirmed, allocated, partially shipped, shipped, cancellation pending and terminal states. Product labels must explain their guarantees. Confirmation here needs durable state, valid allocation and acceptable payment evidence; shipping/capture retain separate substates.

3. Eleven stages: bottlenecks before boundaries

Each stage states pressure, decisions, trade-offs, next triggers and exit criteria. Modular monoliths fit local transactions and small teams; distributed workflows justify their cost only when boundaries actually exist. Service/server counts do not measure maturity.

One store with local transactions

A modular app and relational database keep carts, orders and inventory local for a small team. External payment remains outside the transaction: local payment rows do not establish bank effects. Start with stable order identities, inventory invariants and pending UI.

Problem pressure: The first checkout must sell correctly and be debuggable.

Architectural decision: Use local holds/order/intent transactions and separate external effect identities.

Costs and trade-offs: Shared releases/storage have broad impact; backups, restores and quotas remain necessary.

When is the next step needed?: Browsing competes with checkout resources.

Lost replies preserve order identity; payment timeouts remain pending.

Durable workflows and compensations

4. Peak checkout differs from hot-key contention

Assume synthetic traffic of600K checkout requests in fifteen minutes, eighty browse pages per checkout, and a five-thousand-unit deal with240K attempts in thirty seconds. These are not Amazon metrics. Retries, multi-item/warehouse work and events exceed customer order counts.

Total storage scaling differs from serializing contested quantities. Disjoint escrow buckets can spread work if total budgets and transfers remain correct; cloning full stock into each bucket oversells. Admission reduces wasted attempts while inventory still decides winners.

5. Trace an order and two failure paths

Step through quotes, commits and external calls. Recording results after calls does not close crash windows; participants need stable operation identities and evidence. Durable order state drives UX/support rather than spinners or notifications.

One order through quotes, holds, payments and shipments

Component map; the complete ordered request paths are explained below.

Pending is a real state; confirmation requires durable evidence and intent rather than assumptions about timed-out calls.

Snapshot carts and validate quotes

  1. Send cart versions, addresses and stable checkout identities.
  2. Read an immutable checkout snapshot rather than a changing cart.
  3. Revalidate offers, totals/currency and shopper consent.
  4. Validate promise windows and capacity assumptions.
  5. Use cutoff, calendar and service-level snapshots.
  6. Start retained order identities with immutable quotes.

Reserve and authorize

  1. Persist reservation/payment intent before dispatch with command identities.
  2. Atomically reserve quantities and retain hold results, expiry and generations.
  3. Authorize with stable operation identities rather than reusing one key across different operations.
  4. Return acceptance, decline or unknown evidence; lost replies require recovery.
  5. Convert valid current holds or execute explicit late-result policy.
  6. Persist confirmed order state and fulfillment/receipt intent.

Unknown payments are not declines

  1. Timeouts do not prove authorization did not occur.
  2. Retain PENDING_PAYMENT and the next recovery action.
  3. Query/retry the same operation under provider contracts and reconcile.
  4. Expired holds no longer authorize allocation; apply explicit release/void policy.

Fulfillment and capture by shipment

  1. Publish committed fulfillment intent durably.
  2. Deduplicate allocation/shipment commands and recover uncertain dispatch.
  3. Capture by shipment/amount while enforcing aggregate remaining authorization.
  4. Record shipment evidence and partial state without overwriting cancellations.

Compensate with retries and reconciliation

  1. Cancel with identity and expected order version.
  2. Evaluate shipping/payment state before selecting actions.
  3. Release exact holds under valid terminal transitions.
  4. Void authorizations or refund captures under state/evidence.
  5. Retain compensation progress, unresolved outcomes and support visibility.

          7. Cart conflicts: additions, removals and quantities

          Preserve offline intent under a chosen product contract. Observed-remove membership assigns add tags and records observed removals. Fresh concurrent additions may survive under add-wins; remove-all requires stronger semantics. Union forgets deletions, and document LWW loses independent edits under skew.

          The lab covers membership, not quantity. Quantities may use assignments, deltas or device contributions with caps; choose semantics before data structures. Garbage collection needs causal stability, bounded offline history or forced epoch resync. Acknowledgements depend on reachable durable capacity, and checkout revalidates authorities.

          8. Inventory holds are atomic state machines

          For hard stock, available plus active holds plus committed allocation equals total, extended by explicit adjustments/returns. Commit decrements, hold records and results together; otherwise crashes lose ownership. Terminal release/expiry increments stock atomically and only once.

          Commit under current order ownership, generation, state and server expiry at the serialization point. Sweepers may lag; expired holds still cannot commit. Long TTLs reduce frequency without eliminating races. Late authorizations need void/reconciliation; captured money without stock needs refund/exception handling.

          -- Conceptual local transaction, with command-result identity.
BEGIN;
-- Lock/check the hold and stock in a consistent lock order.
UPDATE holds SET state = 'EXPIRED', version = version + 1
 WHERE hold_id = :hold AND state = 'ACTIVE'
   AND expires_at <= :authoritative_now
 RETURNING sku, quantity;
-- Only if that transition returned a row, increment the same stock once.
-- Persist release command/result and event intent before COMMIT.
COMMIT;

          This is a transition sketch rather than copy-run SQL. Conditional row counts, consistent locking and atomic results/intent are required; never increment after losing the transition.

          9. Workflow durability does not erase external crash windows

          Before effect

          • Persist intent/identity and define uncertain-result recovery.

          After effect

          • Atomically retain local results, transitions and next intent.

          On failure

          • Distinguish definitive decline, transient failure and unknown outcomes.

          Each step retains intent, command identity, participant results, retry policy and next state. If authorization succeeds before recording its result, replay queries/retries the same operation. Resuming the last recorded step alone is insufficient; participants must enforce identity or expose recovery.

          Sagas do not automatically isolate concurrent workflows. Authorities or semantic locks must guard contradictory transitions. Compensation can fail and cannot reverse notifications or dispatched goods. Retain pending repair/manual resolution. Cooperating participants may use2PC; external providers usually do not share that domain, and NoSQL products may support scoped transactions.

          10. Authorization, partial shipments and reconciliation

          Order-time authorization and dispatch-time capture are selected policy, not universal law. Methods have expiry, amount and capture rules or asynchronous approval. Dispatch must define behavior for failed/unknown capture, reauthorization consent and unique shipment capture identities.

          Order caps and shipment idempotency solve different problems. Distinct valid operation IDs can still overcapture without aggregate limits. Void/refund depend on state, and stale events cannot overwrite results. Reconcile order, provider and settlement evidence by identity, amount and currency.

          11. Cells differ from shuffle sharding; overlap differs from outage

          Cells bound serving, data and ownership. Stable routers must not randomly redirect writes across owners. Shared authentication, catalog or payment dependencies need independent controls; shared databases weaken isolation. Gated cell-by-cell deployment limits correlated rollout failures.

          Shuffle subsets reduce shared fate. Two-worker subsets from eight workers yield twenty-eight combinations; two failed workers fully disable one subset. Partial overlaps still fail initial attempts. Distinct bounded idempotent retries depend on deadlines/capacity, so exact-overlap probability is not a generic availability SLO.

          12. Peak days: goodput, fairness and bounded waiting

          Pre-scale capacity/connections, warm caches and test degraded dependencies. Limit useful downstream concurrency rather than only open connections. Bound queues and waiting, cancellation and scoped tokens; queued admission never bypasses inventory. Deduplicate retries and apply abuse/fairness policy.

          Degrade optional discovery before critical ordering, while retaining required security checks. End-to-end budgets prevent retry storms. Escrow buckets need disjoint budgets and safe rebalancing; approximate displayed counts do not authorize units. Measure useful orders, tails, waiting, drops and fairness.

          13. Promises, recommendations and post-checkout lifecycle

          Promises combine warehouse stock, processing queues, calendars, cutoffs, service class and regions. Cache keys need relevant dimensions and validity; stock/capacity changes invalidate them. Checkout retains accepted versions. Multiple packages have separate dates/amounts rather than one fastest-warehouse estimate.

          Fulfillment retains allocation/shipment identities, dispatch evidence and exception handling. Damage and returns create explicit adjustments rather than cached-count updates. Post-dispatch cancellation needs return/refund workflows. Retained history explains partial/pending orders better than one paid boolean.

          Recommendations combine offline builds with bounded online seed merging; item similarity does not require unrestricted pair materialization. Filter eligibility and version data/models with cold-start fallback. Recommendations never authorize prices, inventory or delivery commitments.

          15. Worked interview: test assumptions before optimization

          Trace carts, quotes, holds, authorization and confirmation before choosing hot keys, workflows or isolation for a deep dive. Explain authority, lost replies, pending states and retry identities without prescribing eventual carts or sagas universally.

          Is a standalone atomic decrement enough for reservations?

          No. Retain ownership, quantities, expiry, generations, results and intent with the stock transition. A crash after decrement can lose ownership even when counters appear plausible.

          What if authorization succeeds after hold expiry?

          Revalidate current ownership/state, expiry and generation. Do not commit released/resold units. Retain void/reconciliation; captured money needs a different refund/exception policy. Timeouts do not establish declines.

          Does an undo action for every saga step provide ACID?

          No. Sagas do not automatically isolate, and undo may fail or not reverse physical effects. Guard concurrency, retain repair progress and preserve participant identities across crash gaps.

          Why is a returning cart item not always a bug?

          Observed-remove/add-wins may preserve a fresh concurrent b2 while removing b1. Resurrection of old b1 after losing tombstones is different. Define removal and quantity semantics explicitly.

          Do more shards solve a hot SKU with a million attempts?

          Not when allocation remains one key. Bound admission and authoritative claims. Disjoint escrow budgets can parallelize under conservation and safe transfers; caches/tokens do not replace stock authority.

          Does two-of-eight shuffle sharding imply only1/28 failed requests?

          Only with two failed workers, uniform subsets and both distinct attempts. One random attempt still fails25percent; shared required failure disables all. Combinatorics alone do not establish an SLO.

          Should authorization and capture reuse one order ID everywhere?

          Scope identities to operations, merchants/methods and payloads. Split shipments need distinct captures plus aggregate limits. Preserve same-operation retries and reconcile uncertainty rather than reuse identities across different operations.

          Can a product-page delivery promise be reused unchanged at checkout?

          Revalidate expiry, cutoffs, dimensions and capacity/allocation against the accepted quote. Handle changes explicitly; multiple packages differ from one fastest item. Fresh caches do not reserve shipping capacity.
