1. Indexing and serving are their own scope

Relevance

  • Measure intent, candidate recall and judged quality rather than scores alone.

Freshness

  • Track source, ingest, writer and reader states including deletes.

Latency and safety

  • Pair tail budgets with completeness and current rights.

The crawler builds corpora and autocomplete suggests queries. This lesson designs ranked keyword results, filters, snippets, pagination, updates/deletes and visibility. Public web and private/product corpora require different signals, fields and permissions. Hybrid retrieval can join the pipeline without replacing lexical correctness or authorization.

Define latency by query class, source-to-visible freshness, completeness and relevance. Query latency differs from ingest freshness; accepted writes, ID lookups and searchable visibility are distinct contracts. Specify partial-result and authorization/deletion policies.

2. Fan-out multiplies query work; replicas multiply storage

Assume a billion documents averaging ten KB, or ten decimal TB raw. A hypothetical three-TB primary index is a sizing input, not a guaranteed compression ratio. Sixty-four primaries average 46.875 GB each; one additional replica doubles copies to six TB before maintenance/reserves. Benchmark actual fields and workloads rather than treat shard-size heuristics as laws.

Ten thousand queries/second across sixty-four eligible shards produce 640,000 shard requests before hedges. Routing can prune shards, and replicas distribute load rather than all receive every query. At one hundred 32-byte candidates/shard, a query merges up to 6,400 candidates or 204.8 KB, about 2.048 GB/s at peak before overhead/features. Bounded reranking still needs adequate candidate recall.

Indexing also needs update, refresh, merge, deletion and coexisting-reindex budgets. Small shards add overhead; large ones extend recovery and query work. Measure memory, CPU, I/O, working sets and repair times rather than count documents alone.

3. APIs expose visibility and completeness

Requests define query/filter/sort contexts under server-derived principals. Responses include versions, bounded content, cursors, generations and completeness. Client-supplied scopes do not establish rights; distinguish empty matches from failed/incomplete retrieval.

Updates/deletes have stable IDs and comparable source versions. Acceptance declares durability separately from searchable visibility. Bulk operations have per-item errors, bounds and version-aware retries. Retention/rebuild policies prevent stale resurrection.

          4. Two pipelines share versioned readers

          Queries are bounded, scoped, routed and merged before reranking/hydration. Ingestion publishes versioned readers, while maintenance handles segments/reclamation. Evaluate relevance and freshness/latency in addition to process health.

          Trace queries, cache hits, updates, deletes and rebuilds. Replica/schema/sort compatibility matters through failovers. Stateless coordinators still require coherent contexts, authorized candidates and explicit completeness.

          4. Explain and hydrate

            Explain scores and hydrate authorized fields.

          Indexes avoid many scans without making broad queries free.

          Postings map terms to sorted documents, frequencies and optional positions/offsets. Intersections/unions retrieve candidates; phrase/proximity queries require positions. Dictionaries assist lookup, stored fields hydrate content and columnar doc values support sorting/facets/features. Scoring/filtering can require additional document-field work.

          Index/query analyzers must be compatible, though search-specific synonyms may intentionally differ. Specify normalization, accents, tokenization, language processing and keyword fields. Vietnamese needs more than simple lowercase/space splitting. Version analyzer changes and test multilingual names, identifiers and mixed text.

          6. BM25 is a retrieval score rather than relevance truth

          A common teaching BM25 sums IDF and saturated term-frequency contributions with length normalization. Saturation limits repetition benefits, and b controls length adjustment. Fields, norms and implementation details affect absolute scores. The lab uses exact small-corpus arithmetic rather than reproduce every Lucene detail.

          Inspect postings and score contributions using shared fixture statistics. Shard-local statistics may differ in production, and global statistics have costs. Exact local-top-K merging assumes comparable fixed scores and sufficient local candidates. Changed reranking features can require larger candidate pools.

          idf(term) = ln(1 + (N - df(term) + 0.5) / (df(term) + 0.5))
norm(d) = k1 * (1 - b + b * length(d) / averageLength)
score(d,q) = sum(idf(t) * tf(t,d) * (k1 + 1) / (tf(t,d) + norm(d)))

N: documents in the chosen statistics scope
df: documents containing the term
k1: nonnegative saturation parameter
b: length normalization in [0,1]

          Teaching BM25 with consistent field/statistics scope.

          7. Filter before top-K and authorize hydration

          Retrieve safely

          • Build eligible candidate pools before truncation.

          Hydrate safely

          • Enforce current rights/versions/deletions and escape snippets.

          Cache safely

          • Apply scopes/freshness to cached content and metadata.

          Post-truncation filtering can underfill results and miss eligible candidates. Filter within retrieval before candidate limits, then enforce rights through reranking, hydration and caches. Current-authority checks may be needed for revocation guarantees. Final checks prevent leaks without recovering lost recall unless refill/retrieval explicitly does so.

          Cache keys include effective scopes and coherent query/index/ranking contexts. Protect snippets, counts, facets, suggestions and timing from cross-tenant disclosure. Escape snippets/highlights. Exposed toy corpus text is educational instrumentation rather than a production authorization design.

          8. Durability, refresh and merge are separate axes

          Acceptance has its own recovery/persistence contract. Refresh publishes searchable readers without necessarily making a full index commit. Merges combine/reclaim segments while protecting readers, independently of per-write ACKs. Avoid a mandatory linear refresh/merge/fsync model.

          Versioned guards prevent obsolete events from resurrecting deletes. Retain enough metadata for supported replay or use authoritative rebuild protocols. The lab demonstrates how stale updates can resurrect after refresh; merging bytes does not repair incorrect current state. Invisibility differs from universal physical erasure.

          Faster refresh trades freshness against segments/merge/query costs. Visibility waits or carefully scoped overlays can support own edits, but overlays require correct query/ranking/filter/deletion semantics. They do not automatically establish globally fresh ordering.

          9. Fan-out and correlation amplify tails

          Under independence, at-least-one-slow probability is one minus (one minus p) to N. This is a threshold-event model rather than a universal percentile claim. Shared overload violates independence, and coordinator/reranking/hydration add critical-path work beyond shard maxima.

          Replicas and bounded hedges can reduce delays at additional cost. Budget/cancel hedges and account for correlated overload. Deadlines either fail closed or return explicitly incomplete results; missing shards cannot establish complete global rankings/counts or stable pagination.

          The lab separates analytical probabilities from a fixed four-request trace. Hedge delay thirty reduces its slow request to seventy-five ms with three extra requests; deadline eighty otherwise yields three responses or failure. Evaluate completeness and resource amplification together.

          10. Retrieval, ranking and reranking have recall budgets

          Spend ranking compute intentionally

          1. Match and filter

            Retrieve broad affordable eligible candidates.

          2. Cheap ranking

            Use comparable scores and bounded pools.

          3. Rerank and diversify

            Apply versioned models/features with fallback.

          4. Hydrate and evaluate

            Check rights, content and quality/freshness slices.

          Measure relevant candidates lost at every truncation.

          Retrieval feeds cheap scoring and bounded expensive reranking. Models cannot rescue missing candidates. Measure stage recall across query/language slices and address duplicates/diversity. Feature/model versions, ties and fallback policies affect returned results.

          Web and commerce corpora use different signals and constraints. Behavioral features carry bias and feedback loops. Lexical/vector/model scores are not automatically comparable; evaluate fusion and candidate recall, with RAG-specific context constraints covered separately.

          11. Cursors need total order and stable readers

          Deep offsets collect skipped hits as well as page hits. Search-after reduces this work without freezing updates. Snapshot readers and total sort/tiebreakers support consistent traversal; internal document IDs are not universally stable external identities.

          Bind opaque cursors to contexts and expiry. Reader pins consume resources, requiring limits. Current rights may override snapshot visibility; define that interaction. Reindex switches do not silently mix reader contexts, and expired contexts need explicit restart semantics.

          12. NDCG needs independent judgments beyond returned hits

          Maintain representative versioned query judgments. DCG discounts graded gains by rank; ideal gain comes from independently judged possibilities, not only returned hits. Otherwise missing excellent documents can still score perfectly. Declare zero-ideal conventions and judgment coverage.

          With excellent three, okay one and bad zero, returning only okay yields about 0.131 at two, not one. Evaluate candidate recall, empty results, rights, freshness and latency alongside ranking. Track unjudged coverage and online biases rather than optimize clicks alone.

          13. Reindexing, recovery and resource isolation

          Build schema/analyzer generations from coherent cuts and versioned tails, verify coverage/rights/quality and switch readers explicitly. Rollback policies preserve current safety rather than revive revoked content. Recovery includes checkpoints and deletion/version strategy, not just segment files.

          Bound expansions, scripts, aggregations, response sizes and concurrency. Protect queries from maintenance without starving freshness. Monitor temporary space, merges, reader pins, churn and skew. Malformed-ingest remediation retains identities/versions for safe replay.

          14. Verify query quality together with recovery correctness

          Algorithm evidence

          • Use exact small corpora, contributions and independent judgments.

          Boundary evidence

          • Verify visibility/deletions, rights and cursor contexts.

          Failure evidence

          • Exercise incomplete shards, correlated delays and maintenance contention.

          Trace both visibility and query stages. Slice latency, completeness, freshness, forbidden/deleted-result probes, recall, empty results and cost. Cache hits and score magnitudes alone do not establish quality.

          Test stale updates, duplicate ingestion, missing shards, hedges, disk/merge failures, schema mismatches, expired contexts and cached revocations. Small exact baselines validate algorithms and pagination; missing-shard tests validate completeness policies.

          15. Explain postings through the response contract

          Trace a query with private candidates, slow shards and recent deletes. Explain filter placement, score scopes, completeness, cached rights and pagination through refreshes, then score a ranking that missed an excellent document.

          Must index and query analyzers be identical?

          Not necessarily; intentional search-time analysis is valid when compatible and tested.

          Why is post-top-K authorization insufficient?

          It can prevent final disclosure while losing eligible recall; filter earlier and preserve current rights throughout.

          Does refresh mean a durable fsync commit?

          No. Search visibility, persistence and physical lifecycle have distinct guarantees.

          When is local-top-K merging exact?

          Under comparable fixed scores/order and sufficient local candidates; changed scoring or missing shards alter the guarantee.

          Do hedges always improve performance?

          No. Added load and correlation can worsen overload; measure under bounded budgets.

          Does search-after freeze index state?

          No. Stable readers and total ordering/context binding are additional requirements.

          Can ideal gain use only returned hits?

          Not for full evaluation; independent judgments penalize missed excellent results and require explicit coverage/zero-gain rules.

          Can invisible deletes immediately discard version guards?

          Not while obsolete events can replay; retain enough metadata or authoritative recovery boundaries.
