1. Separate envelopes, message content and mailbox state

Internet transport

  • SMTP envelopes, retries and final acceptance boundaries.

Private mailbox

  • Per-user flags/labels/history and authorized content.

Trust / policy

  • Domain authentication differs from spam quality and authorization.

The provider receives/sends SMTP and serves clients with labels, flags, search, attachments and synchronization. IMAP adds a separate protocol surface rather than a socket wrapper. SMTP envelopes differ from visible headers, and routing/Bcc cannot be inferred blindly from displayed recipients. Shared raw content can belong to independent mailbox states.

Define durability under explicit failures alongside latency, quotas, sizes, retention, regions and abuse handling. Final positive completion after mail data transfers delivery responsibility; earlier recipient responses mean something different. Submission, remote acceptance, inbox placement and reading are distinct states.

2. Logical deliveries differ from transactions and unique blobs

Assume one billion users receive 40 mailbox deliveries and send 10 outbound deliveries daily. That is roughly 463K incoming and 116K outgoing deliveries/s before peaks. SMTP recipient fan-out changes transaction counts; mailbox copies are not unique bodies. Assumed 50 KB bodies, 20% with 500 KB attachments and 1 KB metadata yield 2 PB/4 PB/40 TB of daily logical payload before deduplication, compression and replication.

Metadata is smaller but serves lists, flags, labels and synchronization. Retention, versions, trash, indexes, spools and replication dominate long-term sizing; deduplication savings depend on distributions/encryption scopes. A 25 MB binary attachment takes roughly 33.3 MB in base64 before wrapping/headers, so distinguish binary and wire-size limits.

3. Mailbox APIs and SMTP have different contracts

Remote MTAs speak SMTP; clients use APIs or submission protocols. API acceptance only records intent, with separate recipient outcomes. Scoped idempotency protects submissions. Attachment submission validates ownership, durable upload state and references rather than trusting temporary URLs.

Lists need stable ordering/cursors and declared snapshot semantics; mutations need versions or explicit conflict policies. Authorize content before scoped grants. Search/query/fetch stages preserve access boundaries, while synchronization cursors represent applied history rather than clocks or notification counts.

              4. Separate durable ingress from mailbox/search progress

              Ingress bounds checks and prepares bytes before durable acceptance. Receipts retain envelopes/tasks and protected content. Idempotent mailbox consumers can lag under monitored retention/capacity bounds. Queues are not infinite: defer/reject before acceptance when guarantees cannot be maintained.

              Mailbox authorities coordinate metadata, state, history and outboxes under explicit transactions. Search/push can lag. Blob preparation/publication needs GC protection. Recipient delivery jobs are durable state machines rather than SMTP calls inside held database transactions. This is an educational architecture.

              Two worlds meet at the mailbox

              Component map; the complete ordered request paths are explained below.

              SMTP envelopes/raw messages must be recoverable before final acceptance. Metadata, blobs, search and push have separate ownership/consistency; downstream retries preserve accepted instances.

              Receive and commit responsibility

              1. Exchange envelope recipients and separate message headers.
              2. Run bounded checks under preacceptance policies.
              3. Prepare durable bytes or protected staging objects.
              4. Commit receipts/envelopes and recoverable content references.
              5. Final positive DATA completion transfers responsibility, though replies may be lost.
              6. Deliver accepted recipient tasks with stable internal receipt identities.
              7. Insert idempotently with coherent history/outboxes.

              Read messages and attachments

              1. Request authorized mailbox/message identities.
              2. Authorize current users/delegations rather than trust client IDs.
              3. Read bounded metadata/indexes.
              4. Read or grant scoped access to authorized content.
              5. Return data with compatible state/history generations.

              Synchronize mailbox changes

              1. Commit mutations and durable history evidence.
              2. Project versioned changes without stale-event resurrection.
              3. Emit scoped change hints and cursor references.
              4. Devices receive hints without advancing applied state.
              5. Fetch retained deltas or explicitly resynchronize.

              Submit and deliver outbound mail

              1. Submit authenticated intent with scoped idempotency.
              2. Validate attachments, envelopes, sizes and abuse controls.
              3. Persist sent state and identified recipient job intents/outboxes.
              4. Queue recipient tasks; API acceptance is not remote delivery.
              5. Resolve destinations and handle temporary/permanent/ambiguous outcomes.

              Retry, rebuild indexes and reclaim bytes

              1. Redeliver the same receipt after crashes without duplicating rows.
              2. Rebuild from consistent cuts and compatible tails.
              3. Release references under retention/reader/upload/version checks.

              5. Final DATA acceptance, crashes and duplicate receipts

              Intermediate positive replies differ from final completion after DATA. Bounded checks can reject/defer beforehand. Define recoverable content/envelope/recipient intent and failure coverage; metadata replication cannot restore volatile-only bodies. Early acceptance followed by crashes violates that boundary even if some senders later retry.

              Lost final responses can cause retransmission. SMTP lacks universal idempotency, and untrusted headers/body similarity do not establish unique business instances. Internal receipts deduplicate internal retries; external duplicate heuristics need policies that avoid suppressing legitimate mail. Internal deduplication is not end-to-end exactly-once delivery.

              6. SPF/DKIM/DMARC results, alignment and disposition

              SPF authorizes hosts for protocol identities rather than human senders or visible From. DNS evaluation has limits/errors and forwarding can affect results. DKIM validates signing-domain signatures over selected canonicalized content, with separate signing and author domains; modifications can invalidate signatures.

              DMARC requires at least one passing aligned mechanism; pass alone or alignment alone is insufficient. Published policy and receiver disposition are separate, with local handling. Authenticated domains can send spam, so passing does not grant inbox placement or content safety.

              Strict alignment compares exact domains; relaxed alignment compares Organizational Domains under the selected DMARC discovery rules rather than endsWith checks or the last two labels. Current discovery uses bounded DNS Tree Walks and policy records; older Public Suffix List implementations can differ. aspf and adkim are independently configurable; the lab’s shared strict checkbox is a simplification. With multiple DKIM signatures, one passing aligned signature can suffice. Temporary DNS failures and absent policies need protocol-specific handling rather than unconditional hard failure.

              7. Spam filtering must track false positives separately

              Separate authentication from reputation, content/malware checks and classifiers. Some checks are early, while body signatures/scans need data and bounded resources. Rejections/deferrals precede final acceptance; later filtering uses explicit quarantine/review/failure handling rather than retroactive SMTP errors or unsafe backscatter.

              Catch-rate-only optimization can hide valuable mail. Track legitimate false positives, hard rejection, missed spam and recovery across meaningful slices, accounting for label bias/delay. Small fixtures do not establish calibrated probabilities or production quality; recoverability matters alongside throughput.

              8. Hot metadata and immutable content need reference lifecycles

              Metadata contains scoped identities, ordering, snippets, state, verdicts and content references. Logical ownership routes common queries, while physical partitions need growth/hot-user bounds. Label indexes create multiple rows requiring consistency and repair; bucketed layouts need not be single-partition scans.

              Immutable content can be compressed/tiered/deduplicated within explicit isolation scopes. Hashes are not authorization and shared existence signals can leak information. Prepare/pin bytes before metadata publication and reclaim with fenced reference/retention rules beyond simple counters.

              9. Labels, flags and threads are mutable projections

              Copies can share content but differ in labels/read state. Label archive operations can avoid copying, while IMAP folder operations need correct membership/UID semantics and may differ internally. Concurrent state mutations require version/conflict policies and deletion evidence that prevents stale resurrection.

              Headers assist threading but can be absent, forged or duplicated. Subject fallback can merge unrelated conversations; threads never confer access. Bound/repair cyclic references and expose only authorized messages, keeping original headers separate from canonical internal identities.

              10. Mailbox history and IMAP UIDs have different roles

              Push/IDLE notifications require authoritative synchronization. Custom applied cursors advance only with deltas/coherent replacement snapshots. Expired retention triggers resync, while snapshot/live-tail handoffs need barriers and deletion semantics rather than unions that retain removed rows.

              IMAP sequence numbers can change with membership. UIDs belong to mailbox validity contexts, must not be reused there and can have gaps without missing messages. They are not global history cursors. Adapters implement flags/expunges/moves and validity changes separately from this custom contiguous-revision lab.

              11. Logical isolation does not replace authorization

              Logical scopes in shared physical shards

              • Pack small mailboxes while preserving scoped routing, keys and authorized filters.
              • Reduce tiny-index overhead while checking query/hydration isolation, hot users and tenant work.

              Separate physical mailbox indexes

              • Allow mailbox-specific rebuilds/cold loading at the cost of many index lifecycles and metadata.
              • Still authorize queries, delegation, snippets and content; shard boundaries are not user capabilities.

              Search can use per-mailbox logical scopes packed into shared physical shards. Neither shared nor separate indexes automatically establish security; queries, delegated access, result hydration and content all enforce authorization. Cold-loading indexes requires measured latency/cost/eviction and isolation.

              Asynchronous projections retain versioned deletion/deduplication behavior and explicit freshness. Rebuild from consistent snapshots/tails before generation switches. Sandboxed bounded extraction protects against malicious files, archives and decompression workloads.

              12. Outbound delivery is a per-recipient state machine

              Track recipients independently

              1. Queue

                Durable identified recipient tasks.

              2. Attempt

                Destination policies, signing and bounded transfers.

              3. Classify

                Accepted, temporary, permanent or ambiguous outcomes.

              4. Recover

                Retry, report or reconcile under outcome policies.

              Do not resend known-accepted recipients because others were deferred.

              Persist submissions and recipient tasks before API acceptance, checking sender authorization independently of visible headers. Routing uses supported DNS/MX semantics with bounded TLS/timeouts and destination/account controls. Explicit no-mail domains, absent MX records and transient DNS failures need distinct handling.

              Temporary, permanent and ambiguous outcomes are tracked per recipient/operation with bounded retries. Lost final replies can duplicate remote delivery despite API idempotency. Remote acceptance does not prove inbox placement or reading. Delivery-status messages need loop/backscatter-safe handling.

              13. MIME and attachments are untrusted inputs

              Bound headers, MIME depth, bytes, time and decompression as separate risks. Sanitize/isolate HTML previews and control remote resources. Different parsers/renderers can disagree, so preserve evidence and test malformed inputs rather than rely on upload size alone.

              Prevent open relays and constrain authenticated submissions/compromised accounts. Protect signing keys, grants and credentials. Hop encryption is not end-to-end secrecy; E2EE changes plaintext feature availability and key-management design without hiding all metadata.

              14. Retention, regional recovery and content costs

              Deletion, trash, administrative holds, versions and backups have distinct product lifecycles rather than universal retention rules. Shared content reclamation checks all protected references with fencing/reconciliation. Costs include restoration, egress, rebuilding and cold latency.

              Regional ownership transfers need fencing and explicit ingress durability during outages. Asynchronous and synchronous copies have different recovery/latency contracts. Restore drills include receipts, history, content and key dependencies, distinguishing rebuildable indexes from authoritative state.

              15. Trace accepted instances to terminal outcomes

              Track receipts through queues/delivery, deduplication, references, history floors, projections and filtering. Stage-specific response metrics beat aggregate positive-reply counts, while destination/account evidence protects shared delivery capacity.

              Inject persistence/reply/consumer crashes, missing notifications, stale indexes, expired cursors and reclamation races. Verify recoverability, internal uniqueness, authorization, valid cursors and deletion behavior rather than only liveness.

              16. Questions about responsibility, identity and private state

              Draw two-recipient delivery with a down mailbox, lost SMTP reply and lagging search. Identify knowledge, protected bytes, retries and applied cursors, then consider an independent message reusing the same header identity.

              Does every positive SMTP reply prove durable message acceptance?

              No. Intermediate replies differ from final DATA completion. Protect content/envelopes before that boundary under explicit failures.

              Can Message-ID safely deduplicate every message?

              No. Untrusted headers can collide. Internal receipts protect internal retries while external heuristics need explicit false-suppression trade-offs.

              Does unaligned SPF pass establish DMARC pass?

              Not from that result. At least one passing aligned mechanism is required; policy and inbox placement remain separate.

              Can accepted mail be retroactively rejected with SMTP errors?

              Not through an already completed transaction. Use preaccept rejection or declared postaccept handling with backscatter protection.

              Does user-based search sharding eliminate authorization?

              No. Routing, querying, hydration, delegation and content access all enforce rights independently of physical layout.

              Can revision notifications directly advance applied cursors?

              Not before applying data. Fetch retained changes or coherent replacement snapshots rather than confuse notification knowledge with materialized state.

              Do IMAP UID gaps always mean lost mail?

              No. UIDs have scoped validity and may be noncontiguous; sequence numbers have different membership behavior. Implement validity-aware adapters.

              Is a shared attachment hash sufficient for access or reclamation?

              No. Access needs authorization; reclamation needs protected-reference and fencing checks. Hashes are neither capabilities nor evidence of unreferenced content.
