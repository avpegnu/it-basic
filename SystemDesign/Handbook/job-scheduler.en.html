Designing a Job Scheduler: occurrences, leases and effect boundaries

Version schedules, materialize unique runs/outboxes, recover missed buckets and inspect lease/fencing/DST boundaries while separating occurrences, attempts and effects.

From schedule to inspectable effects

  1. Plan occurrences

    Resolve dialects/zones, policies and stable identities.

  2. Materialize runs

    Commit unique runs/outboxes and safe progress.

  3. Claim and execute

    Use current attempts, bounds and sink guards.

  4. Account and recover

    Record skips, catch up and monitor missing evidence.

Preserve identities and state across failures.

  • Occurrences, attempts and effects have distinct identities/guarantees.
  • Durable progress and atomic materialization prevent silent crash/outage gaps.
  • Lease expiry does not stop workers; sinks enforce effect safety.
  • Highest-seen fencing cannot know tokens before observation/activation.
  • Deadlines, overlap, catch-up and DST are explicit versioned policies.

1. Deciding when to run differs from executing work

Occurrence

  • One intended scheduled instance under a definition generation.

Attempt

  • One claim/execution with its own token, deadline and outcome.

Effect

  • External actions need sink identities, transactions or fencing.

The scheduler owns recurring/one-off definitions, targets, zones, retries, concurrency, cancellation and history. This baseline invokes authorized endpoints or controlled workers; arbitrary untrusted-code sandboxing is separate. Measure due-to-dispatch/start distributions by priority, distinguishing them from completion time.

Choose duplication/miss policies per job. Some work coalesces; other work accounts for every interval and alerts on misses. Arbitrary unreliable remote effects cannot acquire unconditional uniqueness/completeness promises without sink cooperation. State guarantees separately for occurrences, attempts and effects.

2. Modest averages can still fail at synchronized bursts

Assume ten million jobs averaging six hundred seconds between occurrences: about 16,666.7 occurrences/second. Sustained execution with a 500-byte row per single attempt yields 1.44 billion daily rows or 720 decimal GB before indexes/replication. Definitions at one KB each use ten GB raw. Retries, heartbeats and detail retention add substantial work.

A million occurrences at one UTC second assumes an aligned cohort rather than all local midnights worldwide. At one hundred thousand effective starts/second, draining alone takes at least ten seconds before competing traffic. Flexible windows/jitter help only when semantics allow; strict deadlines need separate capacity/admission.

Five-second average jobs imply roughly 83,333 concurrent executions at steady average; ten-second heartbeats add roughly 8,333 requests/second. Separately size dispatch, execution slots, target concurrency and internal writes. More dispatchers do not increase target capacity.

3. Definitions, occurrences and attempts have different IDs

Validate tenant authority, schedule dialect/zone, target capabilities, payload/secret references and policies. Expected versions protect concurrent edits. Generation changes define effective times and handling for existing runs. Manual requests use distinct identities rather than collide with scheduled occurrences.

Run IDs survive retries while attempt tokens change with ownership. Condition renewals/completions on current state/attempt and authority-side lease rules. Effect identities usually use run/step scopes rather than attempts. History and redrive need stable pagination, redaction and explicit intent lineage.

              4. Planners, materializers and workers have different responsibilities

              Planners resolve future occurrences, materializers create durable unique runs/outboxes, queues deliver retryably, and workers claim execution authority. Journals/monitors compare intentions with outcomes. Queue delivery is not schedule truth, and a live dispatcher does not prove completeness.

              Trace registration, execution, recovery, misfires and updates through identity/commit boundaries. Leases do not replace coherent progress/run/outbox commits. Planners retain enough history/progress to account outage intervals; repair regenerates candidates without duplicating logical runs.

              From schedule definitions to evidenced effects

              Component map; the complete ordered request paths are explained below.

              Define and plan

              1. Submit dialects, zones, policies and targets.
              2. Commit validated active generations.
              3. Observe changes and recompute bounded horizons.
              4. Write identified occurrences with UTC/generation metadata.

              Materialize and execute

              1. Read due ranges from persisted progress.
              2. Validate generations and policies at the materialization boundary.
              3. Atomically create unique runs and outboxes.
              4. Publish identified deliveries with retryable ambiguity.
              5. Claim current attempt tokens/leases at authority.
              6. Execute identified bounded effects under supported sink guards.
              7. Complete under current attempts rather than worker names alone.

              Recover paused workers

              1. Lease expiry suspects failure without stopping processes.
              2. Create eligible bounded retry attempts.
              3. A new worker claims authority while old code may remain active.
              4. Sinks enforce supported guards; prechecks alone are insufficient.
              5. Persist exhausted/permanent outcomes and resolution history.

              Catch up after outages

              1. Reconcile expected occurrences under versioned policies.
              2. Restore horizons and classify expired/coalesced occurrences.
              3. Page backlog without advancing past deferred work.
              4. Backfill under fair budgets that protect live work.
              5. Compare expected occurrences with materialized/terminal states.

              Update schedules or cancel

              1. Submit expected versions and effective-change policies.
              2. Commit generations under explicit pending/running rules.
              3. Invalidate/replan candidates with materialization generation checks.
              4. Cancellation races with completion through defined state transitions.
              5. Request cooperative stop with separate handling for accepted effects.

              5. Due indexes optimize range scans without eliminating recovery

              Account for a due range

              1. Read range

                Read from saved progress to bounded time.

              2. Validate policy

                Check generations, deadlines, pause and overlap.

              3. Materialize or classify

                Create unique runs/outboxes or record skip reasons.

              4. Advance safely

                Do not cross deferred/uncommitted work.

              Advance frontiers only after durable decisions cover preceding candidates.

              Indexed bounded range queries can suit many workloads without full scans or universal size ceilings. Time/shard buckets support pagination and fairness; local timing wheels need durable backing. Choose granularity from precision, volume and storage constraints, and shard synchronized buckets.

              Persist accounted-through frontiers and repair policies. Do not ignore old buckets or advance beyond deferred pages. Candidates carry definition/occurrence/version metadata and are checked at materialization. Existing committed runs follow explicit policies. Shard-count changes require migration/ownership handling rather than silent hash remapping.

              6. Unique occurrences and outboxes close crash gaps

              Commit unique runs and delivery intents coherently. Precommit crashes retain pending progress, postcommit crashes replay outboxes, and lost send replies can duplicate delivery without creating new logical runs. Disconnected run/queue/progress writes otherwise create stranded runs or orphan work.

              Dispatcher leases reduce contention without preventing paused old processes. Epoch checks and uniqueness protect mutations, while execution has separate boundaries. Occurrence identities handle definition generations, resolved times, folds and manual intents explicitly; payload hashes alone do not define business occurrences.

              7. Lease expiry does not stop obsolete workers

              Authority creates/renews current attempt leases. Choose durations from measured runtimes and failure/recovery trade-offs with execution bounds rather than nonexistent unbounded worst cases. Distinguish local monotonic durations from scheduled UTC and monitor authority clock behavior.

              A paused worker can resume after reassignment; stale-completion rejection cannot undo accepted effects. Lease prechecks race with mutations. Tokens protect resources only when enforced: highest-seen fencing cannot know an unseen newer token. Immediate invalidation needs coordinated activation or atomic authority checks at the resource.

              Identified effect deduplication needs fingerprints, retention and atomic results. It does not prevent code execution or make arbitrary endpoints exactly-once. The lab compares guard models under both arrival orders and explicitly assumes sink cooperation for current-authority checks.

              8. Misfire policies account for deferred and skipped work

              Enumerate expected occurrences from saved progress/history, applying deadlines and policies. Catch-up preserves intervals, latest coalesces by job semantics and skip records reasons. Bound backfill pages/priorities/budgets to protect live traffic. Use product-specific deadlines rather than equate lateness with useless work.

              The lab at minute forty-five with deadline twenty expires ten/twenty and retains thirty/forty. Cap one admits thirty and defers forty, limiting progress. Latest coalesces thirty; skip explicitly records eligible omissions. Stable identities protect repeated pages. Distinguish policy skips from unaccounted losses.

              9. Cron dialects, UTC instants and DST policies

              Store intended schedules, named zones, policy versions and relevant planner/timezone versions. Tested libraries resolve UTC occurrences; cron dialects differ in fields, day rules and extensions. Preview actual occurrences. Fixed twenty-four-hour intervals differ from daily local wall-clock schedules across DST.

              Gaps lack instants and folds have two. Explicit policies select skipped/shifted gaps and fold occurrences. The fixed New York fixture chooses next-valid 03:00 for the spring gap and distinct UTC identities for both fall occurrences. It is not a general parser; replanning needs policies for existing materialized work.

              10. Overlap policies do not magically fence effects

              Allow permits budgeted independent overlap. Forbid needs atomic active-run checks and later deadline/misfire rules. Replace requests cancellation/supersession under explicit launch policies; cancellation alone does not prove old code/effects stopped. Latest-wins behavior requires cooperating generations or replaceable effects.

              Define overlap scope: individual definitions do not protect shared resources across jobs. Pausing future admission differs from stopping active work. Deletion requires protection against stale resurrection, while cancellation/completion races need authoritative winners and possible external reconciliation.

              11. Retry the same intent under bounded deadlines

              Account for the next step after failure

              1. Classify

                Separate failure, uncertainty and permanent error.

              2. Persist and schedule

                Retain evidence and bounded retry eligibility.

              3. Retry safely

                Use current ownership and supported effect guards.

              4. Resolve or escalate

                Record outcomes and authorize redrive.

              Carry uncertainty through retries rather than discard it.

              Distinguish transient, permanent and uncertain failures. Bound exponential/jittered retries by attempts and elapsed deadlines while respecting target throttles. Preserve effect identities across new attempt ownership. Timeouts/errors do not universally prove absence of effects, and non-idempotent work needs explicit resolution.

              Exhausted work retains evidence, ownership and runbooks. Redriving an original intent differs from creating a new one, which may legitimately cause another effect. Isolate poison work and distinguish dead, canceled and skipped states.

              12. Budgets, jitter and catch-up protect live work

              Separate definition, start and execution quotas. Weighted queues and target limits protect shared workers/endpoints; count fairness may still ignore runtime/byte costs. Strict priorities need aging/reserves or explicit lower-class service guarantees.

              Deterministic occurrence-based jitter preserves resolved times across replans inside allowed windows, with zero flexibility meaning no jitter. Do not mutate identities/times on retries. Backfill shares capacity with live work; scale using lag, age, service time and target limits while exposing policy outcomes.

              13. From triggers to durable workflows

              For multistep work, schedules launch workflows with durable step/result history and data intervals. Recovery resumes recorded state, but activities can still retry after lost results. Each external effect needs its own contract; checkpointing does not make notifications unique. Durable timers avoid sleeping worker processes.

              Intended data intervals differ from execution clocks. Backfilled reports query original intervals rather than current time. Pin definition/payload versions or define upgrades, and avoid replaying old outcomes under arbitrary new code/clocks.

              14. Execution targets grant resource access

              Target invocation needs scoped capabilities and protection against SSRF, redirects and address changes. Reference scoped secrets with controlled credentials and redact logs. Authenticate internal workers and authorize tokens rather than trust self-declared names.

              Audit privileged manual/replay actions. Retention preserves necessary retry evidence while protecting sensitive data. Isolation includes network/runtime/response/resource limits, not merely tenant filters.

              15. Observe occurrences that never produce events

              Monitor horizons, progress lag, start latency, ready age, lease loss, retries, skips and expected success. Unaccounted occurrences differ from failed runs; silence may produce no error log. Independent expected-run comparison respects pauses and maintenance rules.

              Test commit/publication gaps, stale plans, outage buckets, paused workers, sink arrival orders, cancellation races, clock/DST changes, throttles and migrations. Assert policy-accounted occurrences, fenced state and supported effect guarantees rather than equate green completion with uniqueness.

              16. Trace an occurrence through every boundary

              Trace lost queue replies, paused workers and reversed sink arrivals, counting runs, attempts and effects separately. Then recover forty-five minutes of schedules under deadlines/caps and examine fold occurrence identities.

              Is checking leases before HTTP calls sufficient?

              No. Ownership can change before mutation; use cooperating sink guards or explicit uncertainty/reconciliation.

              Does highest-seen fencing reject seven immediately when eight is issued?

              Not before the sink observes/activates eight. Immediate invalidation and duplicate-effect prevention require distinct supported guards.

              Why should effect identities usually differ from attempt IDs?

              Attempts change ownership while business intent remains stable; preserve identified effect scope and fingerprints.

              Can recovery scan only the current bucket?

              Not when older occurrences require accounting. Retain progress and bounded policy-aware catch-up without crossing deferred work.

              Does replacement prove old workers cannot write?

              No. Replacement needs cooperating generation/fencing or replaceable effects, and overlap scopes remain explicit.

              Are daily local nine-AM occurrences always twenty-four hours apart?

              No. Wall-clock schedules and fixed intervals differ across zone transitions; resolve and identify occurrences explicitly.

              What daily history follows ten million jobs every ten minutes at five hundred bytes?

              The exact assumptions yield 1.44 billion occurrences and 720 decimal GB before overhead; avoid presenting rounded-rate arithmetic as exact.

              Do workflow checkpoints make notifications exactly-once?

              Not automatically. Activity retries still need supported sink identities/transactions; orchestration history does not replace external boundaries.
