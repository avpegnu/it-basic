Enterprise RAG: knowledge assistants with explicit access and freshness

Design multi-tenant assistants from requirements/capacity through scoped retrieval, ingestion/access races, evidence/citations, sharing/caches and embedding migrations. Explore principal scopes, in-flight revocation and index cutover.

Trace answers to authority and evidence

  1. Scope / authenticate

    Bind trusted identities, source semantics and tasks.

  2. Retrieve / authorize

    Retrieve with compatible profiles and access evidence.

  3. Pack / answer

    Pack relevant support with uncertainty and citations.

  4. Serve / share

    Revalidate viewers/dependencies and scope caching.

  5. Sync / migrate

    Maintain versions, revocation, evaluations and rollout.

Read-only assistants still carry disclosure and freshness risks.

  • Permission correctness spans source semantics, identity, current access, processors, caches and viewers rather than one vector filter.
  • Prefiltering supports scope without universal ANN recall; postfiltering must precede unauthorized processing where used.
  • Prioritized control events still need revision/tombstone fences against stale publication.
  • Citations require accessible sources and claim support rather than merely valid identifiers.
  • Caches and shared answers need dependency identities and viewer checks beyond question/group hashes.
  • Embedding migrations require compatible queries, authorization catch-up, evaluations and rollback beyond backfill counts.

1. Define requirements and access/freshness contracts

Functional scope

  • Support answers, citations, conversations and connectors.
  • Exclude source-system writes in this case.

Hard boundaries

  • Preserve identities, source authority and privacy.
  • Avoid unauthorized exposure to processors.

Operational targets

  • Declare timing, availability and budget targets.
  • Define behavior for unavailable policy/source evidence.

Design a read-only assistant over approved sources with citations, conversations and managed connectors. Keep effectful actions outside this knowledge path. Multi-tenancy spans identity, source semantics, regions, budgets and lifecycle rather than a prompt field.

Define disclosure invariants and measurable content/access freshness and latency. Bounded revocation propagation is not automatically zero stale access within that window. Stronger current-access requirements need suitable policy checks or fail-closed behavior and explicit in-flight semantics.

2. Estimate capacity from explicit assumptions

Assume 20000 active employees, 10 questions/day and a 6× peak; 5 million documents average six chunks and 768-dimensional FP16 vectors. Assume 2 KB text/metadata per chunk, 5000 input/500 output tokens per answer and 1% daily document changes. These are planning inputs rather than customer or hardware claims.

Thirty million FP16 vectors occupy 46.08 decimal GB, plus 0.24 GB for 8-byte IDs. Index structures, permissions, text, replicas and operational headroom are additional. Compression depends on actual codebooks, codes, identifiers and retained originals, with measured retrieval trade-offs rather than a universal ratio.

The workload averages about 2.31 QPS and peaks at 13.89 QPS, with 1.1 billion input/output tokens per day as inventory rather than one price. Daily changed chunks average 3.47/s; bursts, access changes, API limits and backfills can dominate capacity.

        4. Separate query, ingestion and control planes

        Queries budget identity, rewriting, retrieval, packing and generation; ingestion manages source limits and builds; controls manage access revisions and profile rollout. Separation prevents bulk embedding from blocking access controls while preserving revision/order fences.

        4. Validate output/support and store scoped dependencies.

        5. Source permissions can exceed simple group lists

        Sources can combine groups, nested membership, denies, inheritance, links, attributes and field-level rules. Mirror supported semantics accurately and use authoritative checks where flattening is unsafe. Link possession is not universal public access, and memberships need expiry/revocation handling. Authentication alone does not authorize documents.

        Apply trusted tenant scope across search, processors, stores and caches. Caller-supplied principal strings do not authenticate or authorize anyone, and broad search credentials must remain protected. Authorization must precede downstream data exposure, not only rendering.

        6. Understand filtering, underfill and disclosure

        Eligible-first search avoids restricted top-k crowding in exact rankings. Actual ANN filters affect traversal, shards, recall and cost rather than guaranteeing perfect recall. Postfiltering can protect data under suitable pre-processing authorization while underfilling results; filtering only after models see data is insufficient.

        Overfetch can reduce underfill without guaranteeing evidence or fixing stale authority. Define recall against labeled authorized relevance rather than silently changing denominators. The lab separates fixed ranking, explicit candidate misses and scope.

        7. Handle revocation while queries are in flight

        Indexes and membership caches can lag. Apply current eligibility/liveness contracts before processors and answers. Blocking final text does not erase unauthorized upstream exposure. Revocation after previously authorized processing cannot undo that processing, requiring explicit timing and future-use semantics.

        Unavailable or stale policy evidence needs deliberate fail-closed or independently authorized degradation. Short TTLs do not establish immediate revocation. Track revisions and test changes across memberships, documents, conversations, caches and shared viewers.

        8. Synchronize connectors and publish versioned generations

        Fence builds and publication

          Capture identities and revisioned events.

        2. Build generations

          Build under pinned dependencies.

        3. Validate current state

          Compare content, deletion and access revisions.

        4. Publish / retire

          Activate eligible sets and retire superseded chunks.

        Slow builds must not undo newer controls.

        Use scoped grants and appropriate events, polling and repair. Track source identities, content/access versions, deletion and cursor recovery. Duplicate, reordered or missing notifications require owned observable reconciliation.

        Preserve structure and version chunk references by document generation. Reuse hashes only under compatible encoder and preprocessing dependencies. Positional chunk identities can shift or leave old tails; stage and publish/retire complete sets under explicit rules.

        Fence publication against content/deletion and access revisions independently. Current content can still have stale permissions. Sources without usable ordering need suitable serialization/reconciliation; priority alone does not establish correctness.

        9. Prioritize deletions/access without losing freshness semantics

        Control changes can bypass embedding to update eligibility and dependencies quickly. Membership revocations may originate outside document events and need their own path. Measure source/tenant freshness, backlogs, failures and repair using defined timestamps.

        Slow or polling-only sources cannot support imaginary immediate mirror freshness. Use suitable authoritative checks, bounded scope/staleness or fail-closed behavior. Deletion also involves answers, caches, blobs, logs and backups under actual lifecycle policies.

        10. Evaluate hybrid retrieval, reranking and packing

        Keyword and dense methods can cover different needs, but fusion/reranking require evaluated scoped profiles rather than universal superiority or fixed latency claims. Measure candidate ceilings and task slices; reranking cannot recover evidence absent from candidates.

        Pack useful evidence under actual budgets while preserving context/provenance. Fixed chunk counts are not universal optima across tables, multi-hop tasks and conflicts. Evaluate context selection and conversation rewriting without discarding necessary or using unauthorized historical evidence.

        11. Validate citations, uncertainty and conflicting sources

        Citation contracts include identities, revisions, access and support. Structural existence checks do not establish entailment, completeness or truth. Review source quality and conflicts, exposing uncertainty and versions where needed.

        With insufficient authorized support, decline, clarify or escalate rather than presenting model priors as sourced facts. Faithfulness and correctness differ, and retrieved instructions remain untrusted. Keep unnecessary write capabilities outside this path.

        12. Scope caches, conversations and shared answers

        Cache identities may require policy scope, evidence revisions, context, model/templates and freshness. Principal hashes work only when they represent current applicable semantics; attributes, denies and resource changes can require more. Scope intermediate caches and logs as well as final answers.

        Conversation and shared-answer text can disclose restricted facts despite inaccessible links. Revalidate text/dependencies, deny or regenerate as policy requires, rather than hiding links alone. External copies cannot be retroactively removed by cache invalidation.

        13. Model content, access and embedding identities

        Track source/generation/chunk identities, content hashes, processing/embedding profiles, access revisions, liveness, anchors and timestamps. Content and permissions can evolve independently; timestamps alone need not establish causal order.

        14. Shadow indexes, catch-up and compatible cutover

        Build candidate profiles under content/access fences and catch up declared watermarks. Match query and document representations/metrics; equal dimensions do not establish compatibility. Reuse or mappings need actual evidence rather than universal assumptions about upgrades.

        Readiness includes coverage, authorization changes, partition catch-up, quality/operations and exposure plans. Cutovers bind compatible profiles, retaining rollback dependencies as needed. Processed-row counters alone do not establish completeness or rollback safety.

        15. Operate tenant isolation, budgets and quality gates

        Choose physical/shared isolation by workload, cost, geography and noisy-neighbor requirements under enforced scope. Separate interactive and bulk budgets, bound processors and prioritize controls. Storage/providers and minimal traces must respect data policy.

        Evaluate negative permissions, revocations, stale/deleted evidence, caches/viewers, retrieval, support, abstention and poisoning. Calibrate graders and review feedback. Trace boundaries with appropriate minimization instead of unrestricted secret logging.

        16. Explain failures and trade-offs

        Current checks can trade latency and availability for required authority, while priority events still depend on reliable detection/repair. More evidence/reranking and semantic caches can add costs or mistakes. Choose through measured task/security needs rather than technology lists.

        17. Practice Enterprise RAG decisions

        How do you prevent unauthorized evidence exposure?

        Use trusted identities, correct policy semantics and current eligibility before processing/delivery. Filters need freshness, caches/viewer controls and negative tests rather than prompt-based authority.

        Does prefiltering always give full recall?

        Not with approximation, candidate limits or incompatible profiles. Evaluate authorized relevance, selectivity and end-to-end quality as well as disclosure.

        Is filtering only before the LLM sufficient?

        It can be too late for earlier processors/logs. Appropriate current filtering must precede all unauthorized processing, with underfill measured separately.

        What if deletion precedes an old embedding job?

        Fence stale publication against deletion and access revisions; priority alone does not preserve ordering. Test late work and repair.

        Do valid citation numbers establish grounding?

        No: validate accessible support, completeness and source quality, with uncertainty for conflicting or insufficient evidence.

        Can equal group hashes justify answer reuse?

        Only with correct current policy equivalence and compatible dependencies. Attributes, denials and context can differ; govern the answer text as well as links.

        Can an encoder switch after 100% backfill?

        Not without caught-up changes, compatible queries, evaluations and rollback readiness. Bind cutovers to actual synchronized profiles.

        What if hourly permission sync must support immediate revocation?

        A mirror alone cannot satisfy incompatible freshness requirements. Add suitable authoritative controls, change integrations or limit service with explicit trade-offs.
