Fine-tuning Decisions: choose adaptation using evidence and cost

Compare prompts, retrieval, supervised/low-rank adaptation, preferences and distillation while budgeting parameters/state and maintaining independent evaluation and serving lifecycles.

An adaptation loop with evidence gates

  1. Measure gaps

    Measure tasks, baselines, latency, cost and evidence.

  2. Choose experiments

    Compare appropriate prompt, retrieval and weight changes.

  3. Train and evaluate

    Use reviewed data, pinned settings and independent holdouts.

  4. Operate artifacts

    Preserve compatibility, canaries, rollback and maintenance.

Lower training loss does not establish better product quality or total costs.

  • Choose interventions by measured gaps; prompts, retrieval and weight updates can cooperate rather than forming a mandatory ladder.
  • Weight adaptation can learn facts/behaviors without automatically providing freshness, evidence or access enforcement.
  • Low-rank counts depend on selected shapes/ranks, with GQA K/V potentially narrower than Q/O.
  • Low-bit frozen bases do not make all activations/gradients low-bit or guarantee single-device fit.
  • Preference learning needs sound data, references and evaluations; teachers/preferences are not oracles.
  • Adapted artifacts carry ongoing compatibility, regression and cost responsibilities across releases.

1. Match interventions to failure modes

Facts and evidence

  • Fresh documents, records and access need retrieval/tools and policies.
  • Weight changes do not replace source-of-truth lifecycles.

Task behavior

  • Prompts, examples, schemas or SFT can improve task behavior.
  • Choose using representative evaluations.

Economics and domains

  • Small models, routing, caching, distillation and CPT address different needs.
  • They require evidence, data and operational budgets.

Adaptation optimizes pretrained weights or selected added parameters. It can change behavior, representations and fact recall without automatically maintaining fresh documents, citations or user permissions. Retrieval supplies evidence without query-time weight updates, and adapted generators/retrievers can work together.

There is no universally mandatory or monotonically priced ladder. Domain continued pretraining can precede task SFT, and some retrieval workloads cost more to operate than small specialized models. Measure gaps and compare useful experiments under quality, latency and maintenance constraints.

2. A study aid for identifying missing evidence

Choose gaps and available evidence to identify experiments or missing prerequisites. The result is not production approval or an improvement forecast. Diagnose retrieval misses, compare behavioral baselines and use reviewed comparisons/reference evaluations for preference work.

Unproven teachers supply unverified behavior, while large datasets cannot replace baselines/holdouts. The study rules organize reasoning rather than covering all privacy, uncertainty, hardware and integration constraints.

3. Establish datasets, splits and provenance before training

Adaptation data lifecycle

  1. Collect

    Collect representative authorized tasks.

  2. Review and deduplicate

    Review labels, unsafe targets, families and provenance.

  3. Split

    Separate training, validation and final tests under leakage risks.

  4. Version

    Record manifests and reproducible transforms.

Keep final tests independent of target generation and model selection.

Match production inputs, context/tools and target outputs. Review labels, abstention, languages, edge cases and inconsistency. Quality/coverage matter, without universal sample-count rules. Synthetic examples can repeat mistakes/bias or miss tails and need task-specific review.

Split data before hyperparameter selection, detecting exact/near duplicates and entity/document/conversation leakage. Use time-aware splits for future inputs. Validation chooses settings; repeated tuning against final tests compromises independence. Version provenance, permitted scopes and retention/deletion policies.

4. SFT objectives and loss masking

SFT commonly minimizes target negative log-likelihood conditioned on inputs using teacher forcing. Templates, role tokens and masks determine trained tokens. Response-only and whole-conversation losses are deliberate choices; inspect serialization/masks instead of trusting defaults.

Lower training loss can reflect memorization, leakage or shortcuts. Evaluate held-out tasks, regressions, tools/formats and serving costs/latency. Record checkpoints, rates, effective batches, epochs, truncation, packing, seeds and optimizers, with environment-aware reproducibility.

5. LoRA applies low-rank updates to selected matrices

For W shaped d_out×d_in, use h=Wx+(α/r)BAx with A shaped r×d_in and B shaped d_out×r. Freeze W and train selected factors. Conventional random-A/zero-B initialization gives a zero initial delta; variants differ. Each matrix adds r(d_in+d_out) parameters, with higher ranks adding capacity/state rather than guaranteed quality.

A 4096× 4096 matrix has 16,777,216 weights; rank 16 adds 131,072 parameters,0.78125%. A hypothetical GQA 4096→1024 projection adds 81,920. Selecting q/v across 32 layers gives 6,815,744 trainable parameters. Inspect actual modules, fused shapes, embeddings and any trained biases.

6. Budget trainable state and explicit runtime allocations

The lab uses hypothetical parameter budgets/shapes rather than exact published inventories. BF16 weights/gradients plus FP32 Adam moments total 12 bytes per trainable parameter; an FP32 master copy raises this to 16. These are explicit assumptions rather than universal framework constants.

Runtime allocations are supplied rather than predicted. Target-free LoRA has no trainable adaptation. Dividing pooled state by device capacity does not produce a GPU deployment plan: placement, sharding, optimizer/checkpointing support and measured peaks matter. 7. QLoRA separates low-bit storage from computation

Traverse frozen quantized bases

  1. Store

    Store compact base values and metadata.

  2. Compute

    Use supported dequantization and computation types.

  3. Backpropagate

    Propagate through activations into trainable factors.

  4. Save

    Save deltas with exact base/profile dependencies.

Specify storage, compute and trainable-state precision separately.

Quantized-base adaptation backpropagates through supported operations into trainable factors while storing frozen bases compactly. QLoRA combines NF4, double-quantized constants and paged optimizers in its setup. Low-bit storage does not make activations/gradients low-bit; dequantization, scales and workspaces remain costs.

Frozen bases omit trainable base gradients/states but remain in computation paths to adapters. Checkpointing trades recomputation for activation memory, while paging/offload trades device space for transfers. Single-device feasibility or quality in one experiment is not universal across shapes, contexts and backends.

8. From datasets to evaluated artifacts and serving

Training is one lifecycle node rather than the whole product. Data/evaluation ownership, compatibility, registries and rollouts have distinct boundaries. The traces distinguish objectives and data paths while preserving independent evaluation before promotion.

Trainable weights are artifacts in an evidence lifecycle

Component map; the complete ordered request paths are explained below.

Trace supervised adaptation, quantized bases, distillation, preferences and rollouts. Dataset/evaluation boundaries and revisions remain part of the deployed artifact.

SFT using reviewed targets

  1. Define representative targets and split holdouts before tuning.
  2. Supply approved training splits and intended loss masks.
  3. Load pinned profiles and choose trainable parameters.
  4. Save weights with configuration/provenance manifests.
  5. Evaluate task/regression slices before promotion.

Train adapters through frozen low-bit bases

  1. Load supported low-bit representations/scales and verify compatibility.
  2. Use supported dequantization kernels and backpropagate to adapters.
  3. Batching, lengths, masking and checkpointing shape runtime memory.
  4. Save deltas with separately identified base dependencies.

From teacher to student

  1. Generate approved representative examples using pinned teachers.
  2. Review correctness, diversity and prohibited data.
  3. Create versioned student data without holdout leakage.
  4. Train a smaller student under supported distillation objectives.
  5. Save student artifacts with teacher/data/objective revisions before independent evaluation.
  6. Compare quality, costs and regression slices against baselines.

  3. Pin base/reference compatibility and hyperparameters.

9. What does a student learn from a teacher?

Distillation has feedback and regression gates

  1. Teacher tasks

    Use approved representative inputs and pinned profiles.

  2. Review

    Review correctness, diversity, provenance and separation.

  3. Student experiment

    Version training objectives and configuration.

  4. Evaluate and maintain

    Measure tasks, regressions, costs, latency and drift.

Synthetic labels do not replace independent quality evidence.

Distillation can target distributions, representations or sampled sequences depending on access/objectives. Text-only teachers often provide reviewed sequence targets rather than full logits. Teacher benchmarks do not establish every target’s correctness or preservation of student capabilities.

Collect representative inputs, review correctness/coverage and prevent holdout leakage before student training. Compare existing small models, routes and prompts at realistic utilization. Teacher refreshes, drift, regression work and retraining create recurring costs rather than a one-time teacher payment.

10. Supervision, reward-based RLHF and DPO

SFT: desired targets

  • Use input/target pairs, templates and masks.
  • Review labels, coverage and held-out behavior.

Reward-based optimization

  • Define reward data/models and policy/KL behavior.
  • Proxy rewards can be overoptimized.

DPO: reference-aware pairs

  • Use comparisons, references and β.
  • Review consistency, length effects and regressions.

SFT uses target outputs; preferences compare outputs under matched tasks. A common RLHF pipeline learns rewards then optimizes policies with RL/KL constraints. DPO uses policy/reference log-probability ratios without that separate reward-training/PPO loop, while still requiring consistent data, assumptions and regressions.

One DPO form is −logσ(β[(logπθ(y+|x)−logπref(y+|x))−(logπθ(y−|x)−logπref(y−|x))]). Reference policies and β matter. Preferences can reward style/length rather than factuality and include disagreement; preferred does not establish correctness or authority. Evaluate outcomes beyond training loss.

11. Distillation economics and explicit break-even assumptions

Compare matched tasks, quality/SLOs and windows. Include data/review, experiments/integration and recurring serving, fallbacks, operations and retraining. Student-first cascades pay for the student before fallback; upfront routing and varied lengths change economics.

The fictional 100 K/day,30-day-month fixture uses $4/1 K baseline requests,$0.8/1 K student requests,10% fallbacks and$300 monthly operations: baseline $12,000 versus$3,900/month. A$2,400 setup breaks even arithmetically in≈8.89 days at$8,100 monthly savings. These are assumptions rather than market prices or quality guarantees.

12. Merge weights or retain adapters?

Compatible dense updates can be merged to remove an explicit LoRA branch, but artifacts still cost storage/operations and can reduce shared-base economics. Validate quantized merging, precision and backend support; low-precision merges are not universally equivalent or safely reversible by subtraction.

Adapters support compatible shared bases under engine capabilities but incur residency, loading, compute and KV-identity costs. Enforce exact dependencies and authorization, and benchmark exported/merged/quantized serving artifacts rather than inferring them from training metrics.

13. Base upgrades require compatibility and reevaluation

Upgrade using evidence

  1. Pin dependencies

    Pin bases/modules, tokenizers/templates and manifests.

  2. Check compatibility

    Check loading, export/merge and numerics.

  3. Reevaluate

    Recheck tasks, regressions, permissions and serving profiles.

  4. Canary and roll back

    Promote under gates or port/retrain when needed.

Successful loading does not establish unchanged behavior.

Adapters depend on weights, modules and semantics. Equal-shaped upgrades can change behavior, requiring compatibility/evaluation rather than blind reuse. Not every change mandates retraining; supported reuse, porting, adaptation or retraining follows evidence.

Version data, dependencies, targets/scaling, objectives/references, optimizers, precision, code and checksums. Canary pinned deployments with rollback, diagnosing task/data/serving drift before increasing ranks or epochs. Adapted models are maintained product components.

14. Regression, privacy and permissions need explicit controls

Narrow adaptation can regress capabilities, change tools/refusals or memorize sensitive data. Authorize/minimize training data, scope artifacts and test leakage/regressions. Deleting retrieval sources does not automatically unlearn trained facts; define artifact/backup policies rather than equating index deletion with universal erasure.

Training does not replace authorization, action limits or backend idempotency. Outputs still pass validation/runtime policies. Test adversarial/unknown inputs, slices and abstention rather than hiding important failures behind averages.

      16. Practice adaptation decisions

      Should daily-changing wiki knowledge be put into fine-tuning?

      Define tasks, evidence and access first. Retrieval/tools often suit freshness/citations, while tuning can complement domain behavior. Learned facts do not automatically stay current or obey page access policies.

      Are GQA q/v projections always square for LoRA counting?

      Inspect each module. The fixture 4096→4096 q and 4096→1024 v at rank 16 over 32 layers give 6,815,744 parameters; fused/additional targets need separate inventories.

      Does four-bit adaptation use only half a byte per parameter in total?

      That describes raw base payload only. Add metadata, trainable states, activations, workspaces and runtime costs; checkpointing/offload/sharding trade resources. Validate supported single-device configurations.

      Are preference pairs alone a complete DPO setup?

      Define references/initialization, objectives/scaling, templates/probabilities and reviewed labels. Preferences do not establish factuality or authority; evaluate regressions and length/style effects.

      Does a successful teacher guarantee student quality?

      No. Capacity, coverage, errors and generalization differ. Review targets and independently evaluate the served student against baselines rather than assuming copied quality.

      Does merging adapters eliminate serving costs?

      Compatible merging removes an explicit branch, not inference/storage/operations. Validate quantized exports, numerical quality and shared-base trade-offs.

      Does every base upgrade require retraining adapters?

      Not universally, but blind reuse is unsafe. Verify dependencies and behavior, choosing supported reuse, porting or retraining with pinned manifests and rollback.

      When does a 2400 setup break even at 8100 monthly savings?

      At thirty days/month with stable inputs, 2400/8100× 30≈8.89 days. Nonpositive savings have no finite break-even. Include operations/retraining and independent quality/SLO gates; arithmetic alone does not approve adoption.
