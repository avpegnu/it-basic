Designing Ad Click Aggregation: event time, replay and finalized counts

Trace late clicks through windows and sink revisions; examine deduplication/checkpoints, hot ads, backfills and publication authority to separate preliminary analytics from finalized reporting.

Count clicks from evidence to publication

  1. Accept evidence

    Use durable logs, stable identities and trusted time/context.

  2. Window and revise

    Handle watermarks/lateness, deduplication and checkpoints.

  3. Materialize

    Materialize absolute counts with validated generations/revisions.

  4. Recount and finalize

    Validate coverage/rules/cutoffs, isolated publications and owned adjustments.

Each boundary has identity/time/version contracts; do not arbitrarily add incompatible-provenance records.

  • Event time, watermarks, allowed lateness and finalization cutoffs have different roles.
  • Stable click identities/deduplication horizons differ from replay/checkpoint guarantees.
  • Absolute upserts need version/authority fences so old replays cannot overwrite newer counts.
  • Stable hot-key salting and complete per-ad sums precede global top-N.
  • Recounts know retained evidence under rules/cutoffs; finalized publications and adjustments need audits.

1. Define counted clicks and finalized totals

Event identity

  • Retries retain stable logical identities; genuine repeated clicks and fraud eligibility have separate policies.

Time contract

  • Event times, watermarks, allowed lateness and billing cutoffs are not interchangeable.

Publication

  • Finalized revisions have authority; old streaming writers cannot revive preliminary counts.

The baseline counts unique eligible ad/minute clicks and dimensions, supports time-window top-N, targets roughly minute-fresh dashboards and uses finalized daily publications. Auctions/ad serving and fraud ML are outside scope, but eligibility hooks/rule versions are part of counting. Correctness means consistent deduplication/rules/cutoffs over retained evidence, not proof that every human click arrived.

Streaming may be exact for arrived eligible events but incomplete under lateness; preliminary counts do not imply quantified statistical approximation. Finalization has cutoffs/revisions too: valid post-cutoff arrivals may require adjustments, and nightly batches cannot include future arrivals. Ask whether dashboards and billing need the same version at the same moment.

2. Size events, state and dimension cardinality

One billion daily raw arrivals at one hundred B each give one hundred GB/day before protocol/schema overhead, compression, replicas and archives. Average ingestion is 11,574/second and an assumed fivefold peak is 57,870/second. Partition/worker counts require payload/skew/state/checkpoint/sink benchmarks rather than assumptions that a few dozen partitions suffice.

Two million ads times 1,440 minutes gives a dense grid of 2.88 billion possible daily keys. With one base ad-minute contribution per accepted unique event, nonempty rows are also bounded by unique events, giving the minimum of both bounds. Dimensions, rollups and grains increase rows/updates; count sparse rows rather than preallocate Cartesian products.

Deduplication state roughly multiplies unique rates, retained horizons and bytes/key before overhead. Retaining one billion daily identities at a thirty-two-B budget needs thirty-two GB raw before metadata, replication and checkpoints. Retry/replay/backfill and billing rules set horizons, not watermark lag alone. Out-of-order/lateness can keep several tumbling windows open per key.

3. Durable ingestion and redirects need explicit failure policies

Tracking validates signed contexts, tenants/ads, destinations and payload bounds rather than trusting browser timestamps. Stable logical click identities prevent retries creating new UUIDs. Logging before redirect can reduce lost counted events, but outages require fail-closed behavior, accounted fail-open losses or durable bounded buffering/recovery. Always-available redirects and lossless ingestion do not coexist by assertion alone.

Read APIs bound ranges, grains, dimensions and K and enforce advertiser ownership. Responses expose generations, rules, offsets/cutoffs/as-of times and preliminary/finalized/partial states rather than leaving downstream consumers to infer billability from integers.

        4. One evidence path and versioned views

        Raw logs support replays within retention; archives preserve manifests/offset ranges/schemas for longer recounts. Fast paths deduplicate/window/materialize absolute counts, while recounts use compatible eligibility/deduplication rules at chosen cutoffs. Separate codebases/engines are optional: retained logs and suitable state/migration can replay the same job into new generations.

        Archive consumers must not checkpoint past undurable files. Publish manifests after verified uploads with source ranges and deterministic batch identities. Queries select active publications rather than add stream and batch rows for the same key. Recounts cannot recover raw events lost before acceptance or beyond retention.

        1. Deduplicate before windows/rollups under stable identities and partition policies.
        2. Emit absolute counts/revisions; accepted late events can create new revisions.
        3. Materialize under valid generations/comparison policies rather than blind addition.
        4. Request ranges/dimensions without treating preliminary results as billable by default.
        5. Read coherent active generations for ranges or explicitly report mixed/provisional states.
        6. Return counts with as-of/source/rule/cutoff and freshness state.

        Late clicks and retained evidence

        1. Arrivals after cleanup go to side outputs without silently disappearing from evidence.
        2. Archive logs under manifests/offset checkpoints and verify coverage.

        5. Event time assigns windows; arrival determines knowledge timing

        A 10:00:59 click arriving at 10:01:07 belongs to the event-time minute [10:00,10:01). Processing-time groups depend on network/replay schedules, which can suit operational metrics but differ from business click-time reports. Validate trusted timestamps/skew and quarantine future times so broken clocks do not advance progress across many windows.

        Tumbling windows do not overlap; five-minute/one-minute hopping windows can assign events five times. Sessions merge by inactivity gaps and may extend without policy limits. State depends on active keys, disorder horizons, allowed lateness, triggers and retained data/aggregates, not window type alone. The demo starts with sixty-second tumbling windows and one input.

        5a. Visualize membership: tumbling, hopping and session

        Distinguish unique events from window contributions. For event times 5,12,18,47,55,70,91 seconds, sixty-second tumbling windows receive seven contributions; sixty-second/thirty-second hopping windows receive fourteen because each event belongs to two windows. A window starting at −30 seconds is valid on the fixture’s relative time axis; omitting it would lose early memberships. Summing all hopping windows does not yield unique daily clicks.

        A twenty-second session gap produces [5,38), [47,90), [91,111). Adding unique event34 bridges the first two groups into [5,90), since both 18→34 and 34→47 are less than twenty seconds apart. Previously published session outputs require merge/retraction or replacement semantics to avoid counting old and new versions together. The lab only recomputes membership over a frozen event set, without simulating late merges or sink delivery.

        6. Watermarks are progress assumptions, not completeness certificates

        A minute-window lifecycle

        1. Accumulate

          Deduplicate valid events into event-time windows.

        2. Main trigger

          Emit absolute revisions after progress passes window ends.

        3. Late revisions

          Retained windows can update and emit newer versions.

        4. Retire and side-output

          After cleanup route late evidence into recount/adjustment policies.

        Progress closure manages state rather than promising no future late events.

        Watermarks follow source timestamp/progress strategies, and downstream multi-input progress uses active-input minima. Silent partitions can stall progress; idleness policies improve latency but returning data can be late. Alignment, backpressure and timestamp validation are engine-specific rather than global maximum-time shortcuts.

        Watermark lag governs progress conservatism, allowed lateness governs post-trigger state retention and raw retention governs replay/recount horizons. These are distinct. Accepted late events can emit revisions, while post-cleanup events enter side outputs/quarantine under policy. Side outputs remain evidence requiring reconciliation/finalization rather than discarded garbage.

        With lag10s, P0 max event70 and P1 max40 yield local watermarks60/30 and operator watermark30. Advancing P0 to140 leaves the operator at30 while P1 remains active. Excluding idle P1 advances the operator to130. P1’s returning event50 produces local progress40, but cannot rewind the operator from130; window[0,60) with lateness30 has retired at90, so the event enters side output. Lateness120 retains state until180 for late revisions. Idleness/timeout tuning changes state and lateness-recovery trade-offs rather than automatically improving completeness.

        7. Business duplicates differ from crash replay

        Business duplicate

        • A producer repeats a click ID: preserve its scope and shared deduplication authority.

        Internal replay

        • A log position replays: restore compatible completed checkpoint state and offsets.

        External effect replay

        • A sink output repeats: use a transactional connector or atomic versioned materialization.

        Producer retries creating duplicate log records differ from processors replaying positions after crashes. Consistent offset/state checkpoints address internal replay under supported sources/backends, not whether different click IDs represent one action. Define stable scopes/identities, payload-conflict handling and fraud/repeat policies before deduplicating IDs.

        TTL must cover agreed duplicate/retry horizons or be paired with durable deduplication/recount strategies for longer replays. Slightly exceeding watermark lag does not establish daily/weekly billing deduplication. Partition duplicate identities to shared authorities and detect changed ads/dimensions. Bloom false positives can discard unique billable events, so positive-means-drop cannot prove exact counting.

        8. Exactly-once effects have sink boundaries

        Compatible state/input checkpoints avoid internal replay multiplication when durable checkpoints and replayable sources survive. External writes since earlier checkpoints can be emitted again. Checkpoint-transactional sinks or idempotent/versioned materializations need connector/engine support; checkpointing alone does not make arbitrary HTTP/OLAP writes exactly once.

        Absolute upserts are idempotent for identical records but stale revisions can overwrite newer ones. Backfills/live writers race on unversioned ad/minute/dimension keys. Use monotonic revisions within authority generations or isolated generations with controlled cutovers. Equal versions with different payloads require quarantine/audits rather than arrival-order billing decisions.

        9. OLAP write semantics are engine-specific

        Aggregate keys include tenants/ads, event-time windows/grains, canonical dimensions or validated dimension keys, rules and generation scopes. Use sufficiently wide integers, revisions, source coverage/as-of metadata and publication states. Avoid unvalidated dimension hashing/normalization and summing total rows together with dimensional rollups.

        For example, Pinot full upserts need primary-key partitioning and appropriate comparison semantics; equal comparison values do not automatically provide deterministic conflict handling. Isolate publication generations by tables/key namespaces, compare revisions within generations and validate source conflicts. Verify engine merges/routing/query visibility rather than assuming universal SQL-primary-key overwrites.

        10. Hot ads need stable partitioning; top-N needs correct aggregation first

        Ad-key partitioning can hotspot popular ads. Deduplicate by click identity before repartitioning, or use stable click-ID-derived salts so retries share buckets. Independent random retry salts can duplicate counts across workers. Second stages sum per-ad/window partials, preserving checkpoint/version semantics throughout.

        Unlike disjoint leaderboard members, salted partitions may share ads: local top-N before summing can omit an ad ranked second everywhere but first globally. Complete per-ad partial aggregation before global top-N, or use proven/bounded candidate methods. Match filters/rollups/ranges and expose missing-partition/window completeness.

        For a top-one counterexample, shard0 contains X100,A60 and shard1 contains Y100,A60. Local top-one keeps X and Y and discards A everywhere. Summing all partials reveals global winner A120. Local top-two suffices for this two-row-per-shard fixture, not for arbitrary real candidate limits. If shard1 is unavailable, available-only totals put X100 first but must remain partial; full-data truth A120 is only the learner’s fixture answer.

        11. Recounts/replays publish generations instead of blind overwrites

        Publish recounts under evidence and authority

        1. Pin raw coverage

          Agree on manifest/offset snapshots, cutoffs and rule versions.

        2. Build candidate counts

          Use an isolated generation with matching deduplication, eligibility and suppression policies.

        3. Validate publication

          Compare eligible identities/windows, coverage and divergence; investigate before cutover.

        4. Switch and fence

          Queries pin published revisions; old writers cannot alter active finalized outputs.

        A larger generation number is insufficient: publication needs authorization and queries must select the intended namespace.

        Lambda separates streaming/batch logic and needs shared rules/tests/reconciliation against drift. Kappa reuses pipelines but requires retained input, compatible state/schemas, source cutoffs and capacity alongside live traffic. Neither is automatically exact: missing evidence, rule bugs and bad deduplication can affect both.

        Build isolated generations from raw manifests/offset snapshots and rules. Validate coverage/invariants, compare eligible unique counts, investigate divergence and authorize cutovers; queries pin generations or explicitly expose mixed revisions. Fence old writers from active publications. Final invoices reference published revisions and post-cutoff adjustments rather than mutable late-stream rows.

        12. Retention and observability accompany replay

        Raw clicks can contain identifiers/location/device context. Minimize collection and apply pseudonymization, access controls, retention and suppression/deletion policies across logs, archives, OLAP, exports and backfills. Replays honor appropriate policy state instead of reviving suppressed data from old archives. Keep tracking secrets/tokens out of traces.

        Measure durable-ingest latency/loss accounting, partition lag/watermark skew, idleness, duplicate/conflict rates, late/too-late rates, checkpoints, stale-sink rejections, archive coverage and same-rule/cutoff recount divergence. Avoid unbounded raw-ID metric labels. Measure backfill RTO and live freshness competition rather than average throughput alone.

        13. Test crashes, late inputs and old writers after cutovers

        Validate logical identities and event-time windows rather than successful responses alone. A duplicate overcount and a lost late click can cancel in daily totals, requiring class/window/identity checks. Final counts need traceable manifests/rules/cutoffs for discrepancies.

        14. Interview questions on time and publication correctness

        Track identities, event/arrival times, prior watermarks, cleanup and sink versions. Explain end-to-end failures rather than stopping at Flink exactly-once terminology.

        Does a watermark past minute end prove no late clicks remain?

        No. Source progress assumptions coexist with lateness from idle inputs, skew or outages. Allowed lateness, side outputs, retained evidence and finalized cutoffs/revisions determine recovery.

        Is a deduplication TTL exceeding watermark lag enough for billing?

        Not automatically: retry/replay/backfill horizons can be longer. Define exact identity scopes and durable/recount policies, route identical IDs consistently and distinguish identities from genuine behavior.

        Can additive sinks double-count with checkpointing enabled?

        Yes when post-checkpoint outputs replay. Transactional connectors or absolute versioned materializations must protect external boundaries, including stale-revision/authority guards.

        What if old streaming writes continue after yesterday is recounted?

        Final counts can regress. Publish isolated generations with sources/rules/cutoffs and fence old writers; query active pointers/versions instead of last-arrival rows.

        Is a new random salt on every click retry safe?

        Not when deduplication depends on salted destinations. Deduplicate before repartitioning or derive stable identity salts, then sum partials under compatible checkpoint/version semantics.

        Do local top-N ad partials always yield global top-N?

        No: one ad can fall below every local cutoff yet have the largest sum. Complete per-ad aggregation before ranking or use a candidate algorithm with proven/bounded guarantees.

        Can nightly recounts include every click that occurred?

        Only retained ingested evidence under cutoffs/eligibility/deduplication rules is available. Pre-log losses or future arrivals do not appear automatically; define loss accounting and adjustment/finalization policies.

        Do equal streaming and batch totals prove correctness?

        No. Shared rule bugs or offsetting overcounts/undercounts can hide errors. Compare identities/windows/dimensions, coverage, independent fixtures and publication metadata with owned investigations.
