Designing a Payment System: idempotency, ledgers and reconciliation

Track intents, provider effects and accounting facts through timeouts/retries; explore duplicate captures and balanced-but-wrong ledgers to understand identities, atomic boundaries and recovery.

1. Define money flows before provider integration

Intents and attempts

  • An intent is an order payment intention; attempts execute through providers with their own references.

Financial operations

  • Captures, refunds, payouts and adjustments have identities, amounts/currencies and lifecycles.

Knowledge and truth

  • UNKNOWN means missing local evidence, not proof that providers created no effect.

Ask whether this is a merchant or marketplace, which pay-ins, seller payouts, partial refunds, disputes and settlements are needed, and through which providers. The baseline uses PSP tokenization/rails rather than building card networks or FX treasury. Reliability means preventing duplicate financial effects for the same logical operation under defined contracts, durably recording evidence and recovering ambiguous outcomes, not magical protection against every failure.

Orders, payment intents, provider attempts and individual captures/refunds/payouts have different identities. Clicking retry should not automatically create a new intent, while one intent can have declined and successful attempts. Fulfillment depends on business policies and appropriate confirmed evidence rather than redirects or arbitrary statuses.

2. TPS is only part of sizing; ledger rows and external limits matter

Ten million intents/day imply an average 115.7/second and an assumed tenfold peak of 1,157/second. Three 200 B posting rows per successful capture in the example seller/fee split produce thirty million rows/day, six GB/day and 2.19 TB/year raw. Refunds, fees, settlements, payouts, indexes, replicas, audit/inboxes/outboxes and retention are excluded. Three postings are not universal for every payment.

A transactional database can be a good baseline, but measure locking/hot accounts, durable commits, posting validation, reservations and failover capacity. PSP limits, retries, webhook bursts and recovery sweeps can dominate traffic. Average TPS does not prove throughput is always easy: validate correctness and capacity together under failure.

3. Servers decide amounts; every money operation has an identity

Use safely ranged integer minor units or currency-contract fixed-precision decimals rather than binary floating point. Currencies do not universally have two decimals; fee/tax splits need explicit rounding/residual policies that conserve gross amounts. Browsers submit orders/tokens; servers verify trusted prices, reservations, currencies, merchant/accounts and permissions. A USD 10000 request must not reuse a key bound to USD 5000.

          4. Atomic local facts do not include provider side effects

          The baseline keeps payments/attempts, reservations, verified inboxes, posting identities, ledgers and outboxes in one transactional database, enabling atomic confirmed-event application and durable event intents. PSP HTTP calls remain external: holding row locks through network calls does not create distributed transactions. Dispatch must be durable at creation rather than depend on losable in-memory enqueues.

          Separate ledger services must atomically enforce operation identities with entire balanced posting transactions, track lag and reconcile payment facts with ledgers. Published events do not inherently arrive exactly once: relay liveness, retry retention and poison-event handling are conditional. Establish financial correctness boundaries before splitting services for organizational reasons.

          Durable intent, provider effects and ledgers are three boundaries

          Component map; the complete ordered request paths are explained below.

          Create an intent and execute a provider operation

          1. Submit order IDs, tokens and operation keys; derive amounts from trusted orders.
          2. Atomically create scoped key/fingerprint records, payment attempts and durable execution jobs.
          3. Workers receive committed jobs; replays retain provider/key/payload scopes.
          4. Call tokenized provider APIs under documented contracts without holding database locks throughout network calls.
          5. Responses can indicate success, definite failure, required action or ambiguous timeout.
          6. Guardedly persist references/facts without overwriting confirmed facts with late timeouts.

          Webhook to ledger and fulfillment

          1. Signed provider events may be duplicated or reordered.
          2. Persist verified raw events/inbox identities and account/resource context.
          3. Acknowledge after durable acceptance; do not pretend acceptance with a 2xx when persistence failed.
          4. Process committed inboxes and retrieve current provider resources when events are incomplete/reordered.
          5. In one transaction validate operations, update facts, enforce unique financial postings, write balanced entries/outboxes and mark processing.
          6. Relay committed events; publish/ACK crashes can redeliver.
          7. Subscribers apply idempotently and check fulfillment eligibility; processing does not mean paid.

          Unknown outcomes are not failures

          1. The provider may have accepted/captured when the local deadline expires.
          2. Record an unknown attempt and recovery schedule unless facts are already confirmed.
          3. Sweepers claim recovery jobs using bounded leases and stable identities.
          4. Query documented resources/references or safely replay the same operation while its scope/window remain valid.
          5. Apply evidence; without usable references/lookups, remain unresolved and open reconciliation cases.

          Partial refunds have reservations and their own identities

          1. An authorized refund requests valid amounts/currencies/payments.
          2. Atomically check captured amounts minus confirmed refunds and in-flight reservations, then create refund IDs/outboxes.
          3. Execute the same refund ID/key; timeouts do not automatically release reservations and create new refunds.
          4. Submit refunds against the correct provider/capture references.
          5. Refund outcomes arrive through verified callbacks/reconciliation; not all refunds are immediate.

          Match external evidence

          1. Ingest versioned reports, preserve originals and parse schemas/currencies/cutoffs.
          2. Match capture/refund/payout references with gross/fees/net/time, distinguishing timing differences from missing operations.
          3. Persist owned breaks or authorized corrective transaction IDs without editing old postings to conceal mismatches.
          Checkout

          Payment API

          One baseline database holds operation identities, attempts, verified inboxes, ledger transactions, reservations and outboxes. PSP calls are outside SQL transactions.
          Provider executor
          Uses local work leases/attempt fencing, stable provider keys/scopes, retry budgets and ambiguous-outcome recovery.

          Webhook ingress
          Verifies raw payloads/signatures/account context and persists inboxes before acknowledging. Does not trust success redirects.
          Event processor
          Maps provider facts to correct attempts/operations; atomically applies states, financial posting identities, balanced entries and outboxes.

          Reconciliation
          Matches provider/bank evidence by operations/currencies/gross/net/timing; assigns owned breaks and authorized corrections.
          Provider / bank reports
          Versioned settlement reports have cutoff/timezone and fee/adjustment semantics.

          5. Idempotency is a scoped, retained protocol

          Local keys bind tenants/merchants, operation types, logical attempts, fingerprints and durable results/workflow states. Unique inserts handle concurrent creation, but crashed processing rows still need ownership/recovery; lease expiry does not cancel external calls. Persist provider accounts, operation keys and references early under the protocol so retries do not drift between providers/keys.

          Provider deduplication depends on API/version, accounts/endpoints, payloads and retention. For example, Stripe API v1 may replay cached results including errors while v2 can have different completion/retry semantics; use the actual contract instead of assuming same-key safety for every PSP. Expired windows/provider changes invalidate key-string equivalence. Durable business-operation identities can outlive provider retry caches.

          6. Timeouts require evidence recovery rather than blind new charges

          Local timeouts do not establish that providers did not charge. Record unknown attempts, preserve intent links and do not create new logical attempts merely because spinners are slow. Resolve through documented resource/reference lookups, verified webhooks or reconciliation; replay only within valid same-operation deduplication scopes/windows. Idempotency keys are not inherently searchable resource IDs.

          Without usable references/lookups/evidence, retain unresolved states with ages/deadlines/escalation rather than inventing failure. Do not fail over ambiguous captures to PSP B with the same key: providers do not share global idempotency stores. Speculative refunds do not resolve ambiguity; refunds are separate fallible operations needing references/authorization.

          7. Separate authorizations, captures, refunds, disputes and settlements

          Funds lifecycle

          • Authorized/captured/settled amounts and times differ.

          Adjustments

          • Refunds, fees, disputes and payouts are linked operations, not overwrites of capture history.

          Knowledge

          • Unknown outcomes, scheduled retries and received evidence are separate workflow facts.

          Authorizations reserve capture capability within provider/method windows rather than establish bank-settled funds. Captures can be asynchronous, partial or multiple where supported. Refund amounts/timing have their own lifecycles; disputes have evidence/review/outcomes and can interact with prior refunds. A simple succeeded-to-refunded-to-disputed ladder cannot represent all money facts.

          Use guarded transitions for individual objects while retaining provider facts/events. Old-attempt failures must not downgrade successfully captured intents, but failures for separate refunds/payouts cannot all be discarded after success. Match accounts/resources/attempts, validate semantics and retrieve current resources when needed. Internal UNKNOWN describes knowledge, not bank state.

          8. Webhook ingestion must be durable and operation-aware

          Verify raw bodies/signatures under provider SDK/contracts, account/tenant context, replay/time policies and payload bounds before business processing. Verifying reserialized rather than original bytes can fail. Persist accepted events/inboxes before 2xx responses and process asynchronously; failed durable acceptance should allow provider retries. Keep tokens/secrets out of logs and rotate under supported overlap policies.

          Event-ID deduplication handles repeated deliveries, but distinct event IDs can describe the same capture. Financial posting identities derive from operation semantics rather than message IDs alone. Atomically record processing markers, states, postings and outboxes within the chosen boundary. Preserve reasons/audits for invalid/reordered facts and retry lookups or assign review rather than silently dropping them.

          9. Double entry checks conservation, not business-event correctness

          Each same-currency journal transaction has equal total debits/credits and commits all postings atomically. Account types determine debit/credit effects; debits are not universally money leaving. An example capture posts Dr PSP receivable asset 10000, Cr seller payable liability 9500 and Cr platform fee revenue 500. This illustrative pre-settlement marketplace policy excludes provider fees/taxes/FX.

          An example pre-payout proportional-fee refund debits seller payable by ninety-five percent, debits fee revenue by five percent and credits PSP receivable by the full amount. Corrections append linked reversing/adjustment transactions rather than edit posted amounts. Cached/materialized balances reconcile with journals and still need concurrency controls for available funds. Balanced duplicates remain balanced while misstating money, requiring unique operation postings and correct amounts/accounts/currencies.

          10. Partial refunds and payouts need concurrent reservations

          Two refund requests reading captured=10000/refunded=0 can each request 7000. Separate checks/provider calls do not preserve the invariant. Atomically reserve captured amounts minus confirmed and active reserved refunds by operation. Unknown outcomes retain reservations until evidence/policy resolution rather than releasing early and retrying with new keys. Validate cumulative amounts under capture/method rules.

          Payouts use eligible available funds under settlement, reserve, fee, chargeback and risk policies; credit balances do not automatically imply withdrawability. Concurrent payouts need atomic reservations/spending and operation identities. Compensating refunds/transfer reversals are new fallible actions with potentially different fees/timing, not completed cross-system rollbacks.

          11. Reconciliation is an owned evidence workflow

          Match internal operations/ledgers against provider reports/bank evidence by accounts/references, currencies, gross/fees/net, batches and cutoff/timezones. Successful captures can settle later, while differences between settlement net and gross can be fees. Preserve original/versioned reports and ingest idempotently to prevent rerun duplicates.

          Classify missing internal/external records, amount/currency mismatches, duplicates and timing/unresolved cases with owners, ages, evidence and resolutions. Authorized corrections use new operation IDs/audits rather than editing ledgers to make dashboards green. Reconciliation cannot detect every bug when both sides represent the same wrong intent or evidence is missing; domain validation, invariants and independent controls remain necessary.

          12. Data models and security support audits/recovery

          At minimum store payment/order links, provider attempts, scoped idempotency workflows, financial operations/reservations, verified inboxes, journal transactions/postings and outboxes. Multiple captures/refunds make payment_id plus entry_type overly coarse; use actual operation IDs and suitable posting schemas/versions. Row constraints alone do not enforce whole-transaction debit/credit totals: use transactional posting validation and reconciliation.

          PSP-hosted/tokenized collection reduces card-data exposure without eliminating all security/compliance obligations. Avoid raw card details, CVVs and secrets in examples/logs. Authenticate status/refund/admin paths and apply tenant isolation, least privilege, rotation and corrective-entry audits. Retention/deletion follow appropriate audit/privacy policies rather than a universal period for every jurisdiction/product.

          13. Test crashes between side effects

          Measure unknown/pending ages, webhook acceptance/processing lag, duplicate attempts, dedup conflicts, provider throttles/errors, posting imbalances, ledger/source breaks and reservations. Correlate orders, payments, attempts, references and operations without exposing tokens. Assign escalation owners to aged unknowns and reconciliation queues.

          14. Interview questions on identities, evidence and money invariants

          Identify orders, attempts and operations while marking local commits separately from provider effects. Add a crash and identify the evidence permitting recovery rather than replacing a proof with Kafka exactly-once terminology.

          Did a PSP timeout charge the customer?

          Timeouts alone do not answer. Record unknown states, preserve attempt/key/provider scopes and resolve from documented references/APIs, verified events or reconciliation. Same-key replay depends on scopes/payloads/windows; keys are not automatically searchable IDs.

          Does sending the same key to PSP B prevent double charges?

          No: providers do not share deduplication scopes/stores. A may have captured despite local ambiguity. Resolve using confirmed evidence or explicit recovery protocols rather than blind failover.

          Do equal debits and credits prove the ledger correct?

          No. Balanced duplicates or incorrect accounts/currencies/amounts can still balance. Enforce atomic balanced transactions, unique financial-operation identities, validated mappings and independent-evidence reconciliation.

          Is webhook event-ID deduplication sufficient for ledger posting?

          Not when distinct event IDs describe the same capture/refund. Separately deduplicate deliveries and financial operations atomically with postings. Multiple refunds need distinct identities rather than prohibiting repeated entry types for a payment.

          Can every failed callback arriving after success be discarded?

          No: it could describe an old attempt, another refund or a payout. Match resources/attempts/operations and provider semantics. Guarding one scalar status is insufficient; retain evidence/audits and retrieve current resources when needed.

          How do concurrent partial refunds avoid exceeding captures?

          Atomically reserve refundable amounts including in-flight operations. Timeouts do not automatically release reservations and justify new keys. Resolve reservations using evidence; corrections/compensations are separately fallible operations.

          Does a unique key resolve a processing payment row after a crash?

          No. Uniqueness prevents duplicate creation but neither finishes workflows nor establishes provider effects. Use durable dispatch/recovery, provider reference/key contracts, local work leases/fencing and reconciliation.

          Is a refund a payment rollback?

          Not as a cross-system transaction rollback. Refunds are new operations with references, lifecycles, fees/timing and failure possibilities; ledgers append linked reversals/adjustments instead of deleting history.
